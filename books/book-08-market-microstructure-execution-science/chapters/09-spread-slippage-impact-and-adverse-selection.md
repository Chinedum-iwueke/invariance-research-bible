# Chapter 9 — Spread, Slippage, Impact, and Adverse Selection

Execution cost is decomposed so that mechanical crossing, market movement, own impact, fees, and post-trade information effects do not disappear into one average.

## 9.1 Benchmarks

Arrival midpoint, decision price, prevailing quote, interval VWAP, and close answer different questions. Benchmark choice precedes outcome and matches the parent intent.

## 9.2 Components

Quoted and effective spread measure different objects. Slippage is execution relative to a benchmark. Temporary and persistent impact are models of own-trade effect. Adverse selection examines price movement after fill. Fees, rebates, funding, and opportunity cost remain separate.

## 9.3 Identification

Market movement and own impact are not directly separable from one execution path. Use matched controls, randomized bounded experiments where safe, structural assumptions, or cross-sectional designs, and report residual uncertainty.

## 9.4 Conditioning

Estimate distributions by size, participation, urgency, volatility, depth, imbalance, venue, order type, latency, and state. Tail costs and no-fill outcomes matter to policy.

> **Execution rule B8-EX09 — Cost attribution states the counterfactual**  
> Every execution-cost component identifies its benchmark, horizon, conditioning variables, and assumptions about what would have happened without the order.
