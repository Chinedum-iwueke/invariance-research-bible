# Chapter 1 — The Scientific Role of Machine Learning

Machine learning is a family of methods for estimating useful conditional structure from data. Within Invariance Research it can construct representations, estimate outcomes and uncertainty, classify observations, discover recurring structure, and propose policies. It does not convert association into mechanism, prediction into realizable alpha, or model confidence into institutional authority.

## 1.1 The role test

Before selecting a model, the investigator names the scientific role:

- measurement or denoising;
- representation construction;
- conditional prediction;
- probability or quantile estimation;
- anomaly or similarity detection;
- heterogeneity and subgroup discovery;
- nuisance estimation for a causal design;
- ranking or triage;
- policy evaluation or proposal; or
- monitoring and degradation detection.

The role determines the target, loss, evidence split, uncertainty object, operational latency, and downstream consumer. “Use ML to improve the strategy” is not a formulation.

## 1.2 Prediction, explanation, and decision

A predictive model estimates an observable conditional relationship under a distribution and information set. An explanatory claim requires a separate mechanism or causal argument. A decision additionally requires utilities, constraints, costs, and authority. One model may contribute to all three, but the evidentiary transitions remain explicit.

This distinction prevents a feature-importance plot from becoming a mechanism and prevents a calibrated probability from becoming a position size. Book VII owns allocation. Book VIII owns executable order policy. Hermes owns promotion authority.

## 1.3 Baselines

Every ML experiment begins with a legitimate non-ML or low-complexity comparator: constant forecast, historical mean, simple rule, linear or logistic model, monotone score, nearest prior policy, or existing production baseline. The comparator uses the same information, labels, split, cost treatment, and metric.

A complex model earns attention by improving a decision-relevant property that survives uncertainty, drift, costs, and operational constraints. Training loss or headline accuracy is insufficient.

## 1.4 Epistemic status

Model output is evidence with scope. It inherits data construction, sample selection, label quality, split validity, hyperparameter search, calibration, and deployment shift. Learned parameters are fitted artifacts; embeddings and clusters are hypotheses about representation; latent regimes are model-dependent descriptions.

> **ML rule B6-ML01 — Learning does not transfer authority**  
> A model may estimate, rank, or propose within its contract; it cannot promote itself, change protected evidence access, allocate capital, or mutate production policy.

## 1.5 Acceptance

ML is scientifically warranted when the role is defined, simpler competitors are credible, temporal and selection biases are controlled, uncertainty is useful, degradation is monitorable, and the output improves the intended decision under realistic constraints. Otherwise the correct result may be that ML is unnecessary.
