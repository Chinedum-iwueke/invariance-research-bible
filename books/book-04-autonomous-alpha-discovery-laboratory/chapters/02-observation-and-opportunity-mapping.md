# Chapter 2 — Observation and Opportunity Mapping

Discovery begins by mapping observations to explicit expectations and unresolved decisions. Opportunity mapping organizes where inquiry may be valuable; it does not label unusual market behavior as tradable.

## 2.1 Observation records

An observation records object, representation, time and availability, population, measurement, expected behavior if any, deviation, uncertainty, provenance, and search context. It may arise from markets, models, execution, risk, infrastructure, literature, or contradiction between institutional records.

Raw event, derived metric, model residual, alert, and human report remain distinct. A detector score links to its method and calibration. Operational alerts can require containment even when scientific interpretation is unknown.

## 2.2 Expectations

An anomaly requires an expectation supplied by a model, mechanism, benchmark, prior distribution, operational contract, or institutional belief. The expectation states scope and tolerance. Without it, a large movement is salient but not formally anomalous.

Expectations can be wrong. The first investigation asks whether the observation, representation, or expectation failed before proposing an opportunity.

> **Discovery rule B4-DR02 — Observation does not imply opportunity**  
> A deviation enters investigation with artifact and alternative explanations; it cannot become a hypothesis, signal, or candidate through detector magnitude alone.

## 2.3 Opportunity records

An opportunity record is a governed assessment that resolving a question may create scientific, operational, portfolio, or strategic value. It references the observation or knowledge gap, candidate questions, nearest prior work, plausible mechanisms, feasibility, required evidence, cost, risk, and uncertainty.

Opportunity value can include avoiding repeated work, validating infrastructure, resolving a portfolio dependency, or learning that a celebrated effect is unusable. Expected profit is one uncertain component, not the universal unit.

## 2.4 Mapping dimensions

Opportunity mapping spans market, instrument, venue, horizon, participant, mechanism, observable information, representation, target, method, execution condition, portfolio dependency, and institutional capability. The dimensions prevent a flat list of “alpha ideas” from mixing causal sources, extraction methods, and operational improvements.

Mappings are sparse and provisional. Missing combinations may reflect impossibility, irrelevance, lack of evidence, or unexplored space. Graph absence does not establish novelty.

## 2.5 Prior-work comparison

Before allocating material search, Research Intelligence retrieves similar questions, claims, experiments, failures, mechanisms, representations, and portfolio exposures. A novelty report identifies nearest prior objects and the material difference along each dimension.

New wording is not novelty. A different model estimating the same target from the same information may be method novelty without informational novelty. Low return correlation does not prove a different mechanism.

## 2.6 Prioritization interface

Research proposes scientific value and tractability. Knowledge provides evidence and gaps. Engineering estimates data and compute feasibility. Governance applies policy and protected-evidence constraints. Portfolio and Production authorities provide relevance and operational risk where applicable.

The resulting priority is a decision under uncertainty. Opportunity maps supply reasons and dependencies; Hermes authorizes the program and budget.
