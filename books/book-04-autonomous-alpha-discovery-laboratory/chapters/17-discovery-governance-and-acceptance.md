# Chapter 17 — Discovery Governance and Acceptance

The discovery laboratory is accepted when it can expand scientific search while preserving evidence, authority, and stopping. A demonstration that generates an attractive hypothesis is insufficient. Acceptance exercises the complete loop and its adversarial failure paths.

## 17.1 Minimum accepted loop

An accepted loop can:

1. ingest an observation or knowledge gap;
2. retrieve prior work, contradiction, and failures;
3. form a scoped question and alternatives;
4. produce a registration-ready hypothesis contract;
5. define a typed search space and budget;
6. execute material attempts through deterministic laboratory services;
7. preserve search and protected-evidence history;
8. perform independent adversarial review;
9. close every branch with a disposition;
10. publish resulting evidence and questions to Research Intelligence.

The accepted loop need not produce realizable alpha.

## 17.2 Governance tests

Tests attempt self-expansion, self-promotion, holdout bypass, budget evasion, hidden retries, package mutation, unauthorized graph inference, stale charter use, and continuation after stop. They also verify legitimate amendment, escalation, cancellation, and recovery.

> **Discovery rule B4-DR17 — Acceptance includes failure paths**  
> The laboratory is not accepted until invalid data, tool failure, exhausted budget, unavailable evidence, adversarial disagreement, and zero-candidate sessions terminate safely with complete lineage.

## 17.3 Scientific evaluation

Evaluation fixtures include known duplicates, planted leakage, spurious correlations, genuine but narrow relationships, null spaces, mechanism alternatives, and representation defects. Judges assess grounding, novelty relative to corpus, testability, selection accounting, and interpretation.

Search methods are compared under declared spaces and budgets. LLM and agent evaluations include the denominator of generated and viewed proposals, not selected showcases.

## 17.4 Operational evaluation

The system verifies identity, permissions, tool sandboxing, deterministic artifacts, event lineage, observability, cancellation, checkpoint recovery, rate and cost budgets, and contribution to Research Intelligence. Sensitive context and credentials remain excluded from general traces.

## 17.5 Acceptance dossier

The dossier records scope, versions, source and evaluation fixtures, test plan, raw results, defects, exceptions, residual risks, excluded capabilities, accountable approval, and expiry. Any unimplemented component remains a proposal.

Capability expansion—new search languages, models, data classes, environments, or autonomous actions—requires impact review and regression evidence.

## 17.6 Boundary to later books

Book V demonstrates deterministic experimental execution. Book VI evaluates learned models. Books VII–VIII determine portfolio and execution relevance. Book IX constructs workers. Book X deploys the integrated system.

Book IV succeeds when these consumers receive honest, registration-ready scientific objects whose search lineage can withstand criticism.
