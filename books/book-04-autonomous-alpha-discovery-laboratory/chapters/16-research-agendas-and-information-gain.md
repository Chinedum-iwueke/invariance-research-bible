# Chapter 16 — Research Agendas and Information Gain

The research agenda allocates scarce attention, evidence access, engineering, compute, review, and capital-bearing experimentation among questions. It is a governed portfolio of inquiry, not a queue sorted by a model’s estimate of alpha.

## 16.1 Agenda objects

An agenda item references a question or capability need, evidence gap, nearest prior work, expected outcomes, decision relevance, required resources, dependencies, risks, protected evidence, owner, horizon, and stopping condition. It can represent discovery, replication, falsification, infrastructure, incident follow-up, or corpus repair.

Items preserve deferred and rejected history so priority changes remain explainable.

## 16.2 Expected information gain

Expected information gain can express the anticipated reduction in decision-relevant uncertainty from possible outcomes. It requires a current belief, outcome model, update rule, and utility of resolving uncertainty. These quantities are estimates, often coarse and contested.

The formal information-theoretic quantity is used only when variables and distributions are specified. Ordinary “learning value” is not mislabeled as Shannon information.

> **Discovery rule B4-DR16 — Priority remains judgment**  
> Information gain, novelty, expected economic value, urgency, feasibility, cost, risk, and diversification are attributable decision inputs; no scalar score acquires research-budget authority.

## 16.3 Portfolio balance

Agenda review considers concentration across markets, mechanisms, horizons, representations, methods, data sources, and decision types. Diversity reduces some shared failure and expands learning, but variety without quality is not an objective.

Infrastructure and replication compete fairly with candidate generation. A data-lineage repair can have higher institutional value than another strategy search.

## 16.4 Sequential allocation

Programs can receive staged budgets. Early work resolves feasibility or major uncertainty; later work follows only if prespecified evidence justifies it. Value-of-information reasoning can help choose the next experiment, while selection and stopping remain in search history.

Sunk cost is not evidence for continuation. Cancellation preserves artifacts and lessons.

## 16.5 Inputs and authority

Research proposes questions and scientific value. Knowledge supplies prior evidence, contradictions, and gaps. Engineering estimates data and execution feasibility. Governance enforces constraints. Portfolio and Production contribute relevance and operational consequence.

Hermes authorizes the agenda, budgets, and exceptions. Research Intelligence maintains the evidence projection but cannot schedule itself.

## 16.6 Agenda review

Review examines learning achieved, beliefs changed or bounded, duplication avoided, resource use, search exposure, unresolved risk, and value of continuation relative to alternatives. Candidate count and headline backtest performance are not agenda-success metrics.

The Continuous Research Agenda changes through attributable decisions and remains recoverable at each effective date.
