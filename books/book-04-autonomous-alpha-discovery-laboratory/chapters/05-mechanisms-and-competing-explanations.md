# Chapter 5 — Mechanisms and Competing Explanations

A pattern becomes more scientifically useful when it generates explanations that can disagree about future observations. Mechanism work organizes those explanations, identifies boundaries, and designs discriminating tests. It does not turn plausible market stories into causal evidence.

## 5.1 Mechanism records

A mechanism record states the actors or processes, incentives and constraints, information flow, temporal sequence, predicted relationship, boundary conditions, possible interventions, and evidence for and against. It distinguishes observed components from inferred links.

Market mechanisms may concern inventory, liquidity provision, forced flow, information acquisition, attention, institutional rules, risk transfer, latency, funding, collateral, or adaptation. A predictive rationale can remain useful when a full causal account is unavailable, but its claim type stays predictive.

## 5.2 Alternative sets

Every material hypothesis names credible alternatives. These include market explanations and artifact explanations such as leakage, stale observations, survivorship, benchmark exposure, data revisions, search selection, cost omission, and implementation error.

The alternative set is prioritized by plausibility and consequence rather than padded with trivial straw positions. Research Intelligence retrieves prior critiques and related failures; researchers add domain-specific accounts.

> **Discovery rule B4-DR05 — Mechanisms must discriminate**  
> A proposed mechanism identifies at least one observation, boundary, or intervention under which its implications differ materially from credible alternatives.

## 5.3 Mechanism graphs

Mechanism graphs link actors, states, constraints, actions, observables, and outcomes through typed observed, causal-assumption, and predicted edges. They are reviewed hypothesis structures, not established causal graphs by storage.

Graph traversal can reveal missing mediators, shared dependencies, and conflicting predictions. Centrality or path plausibility never validates a mechanism.

## 5.4 Predictive, explanatory, and causal evidence

Predictive evidence shows conditional information under a design. Explanatory evidence supports a process and its discriminating implications. Causal evidence supports an intervention or counterfactual under an identification strategy. One study can contribute to several types, but the bridges are argued explicitly.

A relationship can proceed to bounded predictive use without complete causal identification when prospective evidence and controls justify it. Mechanism uncertainty remains part of monitoring and transport risk.

## 5.5 Tests and boundary probes

Mechanism tests vary conditions that the account says should matter: participant constraints, timing, liquidity, venue rules, information arrival, horizon, or market state. Negative controls test variables or intervals where no effect is expected. Placebo timing and alternative benchmarks can expose artifacts.

Observed boundary failure can narrow rather than destroy a mechanism. Post-result boundary discovery begins a new registered claim.

## 5.6 Synthesis

Mechanism review produces a comparison table of assumptions, predictions, evidence, unresolved conflicts, and decision sensitivity. It may select a leading account, preserve several, or conclude that prediction is supported while explanation is unresolved.

The value is not narrative completeness. It is a sharper experiment and a more honest statement of what can travel beyond the observed sample.
