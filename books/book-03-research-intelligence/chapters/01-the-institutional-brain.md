# Chapter 1 — The Institutional Brain

Research Intelligence is the institutional brain, corpus, reasoning substrate, and long-term memory of Invariance Research. It preserves what the institution has read, observed, attempted, decided, learned, disputed, and forgotten under governance. It allows a future task to recover applicable evidence without depending on the memory of a particular person, model, or software generation.

The metaphor “brain” describes function, not autonomy or anatomy. Research Intelligence does not become the institution, the operating authority, or a sentient actor. Hermes coordinates governed work. Accountable organizations decide. Bulletproof BT produces deterministic experimental evidence. Research Intelligence supplies durable cognition across them.

## 1.1 Cognition outside the model

A model runtime is a replaceable inference engine. Its weights, context window, and conversation history are not canonical memory. Models can summarize, retrieve, compare, formalize, propose, and criticize, but their outputs remain typed claims with provenance and review state.

Durable cognition resides in institutional objects: sources, concepts, claims, assumptions, methods, datasets, representations, experiments, decisions, incidents, beliefs, contradictions, and their lineage. A new runtime can use these objects without inheriting undocumented private context from its predecessor.

> **Knowledge rule B3-KR01 — Memory remains institutional**  
> No individual, agent, model runtime, vendor, index, or graph projection may be the sole repository of evidence or reasoning required to reconstruct a material institutional decision.

## 1.2 Four logical planes

Research Intelligence can be understood through four logical planes:

- the **institutional substrate** holds canonical identities, sources, evidence, memory, lineage, and access metadata;
- the **reasoning plane** assembles context and coordinates replaceable cognitive services;
- the **analytical plane** invokes deterministic parsers, search, mathematics, statistics, code, and validation tools;
- the **control plane** enforces identity, policy, budgets, permissions, and contribution rules through Hermes interfaces.

These are responsibility boundaries, not required network services. A local implementation can combine them physically while retaining separate contracts and authority.

## 1.3 What Research Intelligence does

Its durable capabilities include source recovery, provenance, scientific-object extraction, hybrid retrieval, evidence dossier compilation, citation replay, research episode memory, scientific lineage, belief records, graph projections, cognitive-tool coordination, corpus governance, and evaluation.

It also exposes gaps. A trustworthy system reports missing evidence, failed extraction, inaccessible sources, unresolved contradiction, and uncertainty. Retrieval fluency must not conceal absence.

## 1.4 What it does not do

Research Intelligence does not promote candidates, allocate compute or capital, change policy, submit orders, or declare a hypothesis true. It can provide an evidence-linked assessment or proposal; Hermes routes the decision to the accountable authority.

It is not a universal answer engine. Some questions require new experiments, specialist judgment, protected data, or an explicit decision under uncertainty. A dossier can clarify the boundary without removing it.

## 1.5 The closed knowledge loop

Before governed work, Research Intelligence retrieves applicable doctrine, prior evidence, failures, contradictions, and unresolved questions. During work, it supplies task-scoped context and deterministic tools. After work, it ingests the resulting artifacts, decision, and disposition. Later tasks can retrieve the episode.

The loop is complete when evidence changes the institutional record, including a justified non-change or an invalidity finding. Artifact volume alone is not learning.

## 1.6 Replaceability and evolution

Parsers, embedding models, rerankers, language models, graph algorithms, and storage technologies will change. Their versions remain part of provenance. Derived indexes and projections are rebuildable; canonical source and evidence identity persists.

Evolution is accepted through regression evidence. A newer model does not replace an older service merely because it is marketed as more capable. Retrieval, citation, reasoning, security, calibration, cost, and degraded behavior are evaluated in institutional context.

Research Intelligence earns the name “brain” by sustaining coordinated memory and reasoning across replacement. Its authority comes from controlled evidence and lineage, never from the apparent intelligence of one runtime.
