# Chapter 2 — Constitutional Reasoning and Context

Research Intelligence must reason inside the institution’s adopted constraints without pretending that prose compiles itself into unambiguous policy. Constitutional context supplies doctrine, definitions, authority, and active decisions to a bounded task. Hermes remains responsible for enforcement and adjudication.

## 2.1 Context layers

A task context draws from four layers:

- **constitutional context** — applicable doctrine, controlled terminology, architecture decisions, and non-waivable boundaries;
- **institutional context** — current policy, roles, programs, candidate states, decisions, and effective exceptions;
- **scientific context** — sources, claims, methods, evidence, contradictions, and beliefs relevant to the question;
- **task context** — the charter, inputs, tools, budget, output contract, and work completed so far.

Reviewer context may be deliberately separated from originator context to protect independent evaluation. The separation is recorded rather than assumed.

## 2.2 Context manifests

Every consequential reasoning task receives a context manifest identifying selected objects, versions, retrieval process, policy time, access boundary, and known omissions. The manifest permits reconstruction without storing unrestricted private thought or secret-bearing prompts.

Selection itself is consequential. Excluding a contradiction or using an obsolete policy can change the result. The manifest records automatic and human selections, truncation, redaction, and unavailable objects.

> **Knowledge rule B3-KR02 — Context is evidence-bearing**  
> A material reasoning output identifies the constitutional, institutional, scientific, and task context that constrained it, including material omissions and access limitations.

## 2.3 Constitutional conformance

A conformance service can retrieve relevant doctrine, detect explicit conflicts, and generate a structured assessment. It cannot resolve every ambiguity. When two rules appear inconsistent, the task escalates with the cited provisions, object, proposed action, and interpretive alternatives.

Within a task, the constitutional snapshot is fixed. The canon can change only through its governed process. If a canonical change occurs during long-running work, policy determines whether the task continues under its original snapshot or restarts under the new one.

## 2.4 Policy and knowledge

Constitutional Knowledge governs institutional commitments. Operational policy is a versioned rule adopted under that authority. Scientific Knowledge can motivate policy change but does not amend policy automatically. Institutional evidence can reveal that a control fails without itself authorizing the replacement.

Research Intelligence preserves these relationships. Hermes executes policy and records decisions. This separation prevents retrieval ranking or a model interpretation from becoming hidden governance.

## 2.5 Output discipline

Reasoning outputs label doctrine, implementation fact, empirical result, proposal, and inference where ambiguity could matter. They cite primary evidence and identify assumptions. An output may recommend accept, revise, reject, or escalate, but these are recommendations or workflow dispositions unless a domain policy grants the exact decision right.

Domain states remain authoritative. A source can be ingested but quarantined; evidence can be admissible but weak; a claim can be disputed; an action can be unauthorized. Four generic verdict words cannot replace these semantics.

## 2.6 Minimal and sufficient context

More context is not always better. Large undifferentiated contexts obscure authority, increase leakage risk, and consume attention. Research Intelligence retrieves the smallest set sufficient to support the task, with expandable links to parent structure and opposition.

Protected or personal content is minimized and purpose-bound. Derived summaries retain restrictions. The system records that relevant evidence was unavailable without revealing its protected content through metadata or error detail.
