# Chapter 8 — Concepts, Claims, Methods, and Assumptions

Scientific reasoning becomes unreliable when definitions, empirical claims, methods, assumptions, and interpretations are stored as undifferentiated passages. Research Intelligence extracts and links typed scientific objects while preserving their exact source context and uncertainty.

## 8.1 Concepts and definitions

A concept has identity, preferred term, definition, scope, aliases, relationships, examples, counterexamples, source, and review state. Different disciplines or authors may use the same word differently; the system preserves scoped definitions rather than forcing premature synonymy.

Controlled institutional terminology is adopted through governance. An extracted source definition remains Scientific Knowledge and does not silently change the glossary.

## 8.2 Claims and claim types

A claim record states normalized proposition, source wording, claimant, scope, time, modality, claim type, supporting and opposing evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, and status. Relevant types include mathematical identity, theorem under premises, external empirical claim, internal empirical result, implementation fact, model output, agent inference, institutional belief, proposal, dispute, unknown, and retired claim.

Normalization aids comparison but does not replace the quoted or paraphrased source span. Two similar sentences can differ materially in population, horizon, or causal strength.

> **Knowledge rule B3-KR08 — Type before synthesis**  
> Research Intelligence identifies the epistemic and evidential type of a scientific object before combining, ranking, or using it in a dossier; semantic similarity cannot erase claim scope or authority.

## 8.3 Assumptions

Assumptions can be definitional, mathematical, statistical, causal, measurement, institutional, operational, or environmental. They link to the claims and methods that depend on them and to evidence that tests or challenges them.

An unstated assumption inferred by a model is labeled as inference until reviewed. Violated assumptions can invalidate a method without falsifying the underlying question.

## 8.4 Methods

A method object records purpose, problem class, estimand or target, inputs, assumptions, procedure, diagnostics, outputs, uncertainty semantics, alternatives, failure modes, implementations, and evidence of use. It is not merely an algorithm name.

Method applicability is a claim. A standard method can be inappropriate under dependence, selection, censoring, non-stationarity, or limited sample information. Cognitive services retrieve and compare evidence; domain books own detailed methodology.

## 8.5 Variables and measurements

Variables record definition, units, domain, representation, time semantics, availability, transformation, and role in a claim or method. Similar names do not establish equivalent measurements. Targets and outcomes preserve horizon, censoring, benchmark, and accounting conventions.

This structure supports dimensional checks, formula reconciliation, dataset compatibility, and cross-study comparison without assuming interchangeability.

## 8.6 Relationships and contradiction

Objects link through defines, assumes, measures, estimates, supports, weakens, contradicts, instantiates, derives, applies-to, and supersedes relationships. Every relationship has provenance and review state.

Contradiction requires comparable scope. A result in another population may reveal a boundary rather than directly refute a claim. Chapter 10 develops deliberate contradiction retrieval.

## 8.7 Extraction and review

Automated extraction proposes objects with source coordinates, model or rule provenance, and confidence. Deterministic validators check identifiers, units, citations, formulas, and schema. Human or governed review focuses on consequential ambiguity.

Accepted extraction means faithful representation of the source, not endorsement of the claim. That distinction allows the corpus to preserve error, disagreement, and historical belief without converting them into current institutional truth.
