# Chapter 18 — Evaluation, Operations, and Evolution

Research Intelligence is trustworthy only to the extent that its recovery, retrieval, citation, reasoning, permissions, memory, and degraded behavior are evaluated against identified uses. Component benchmarks are necessary but do not prove that the institutional loop works.

## 18.1 Recovery evaluation

Recovery evaluation samples metadata, reading order, hierarchy, quotations, equations, tables, figures, citations, and coordinates by source type and quality. Gold objects have independent judgments, adjudication, provenance, and versioning. Character accuracy alone cannot establish scientific usability.

## 18.2 Retrieval and citation evaluation

Retrieval evaluation measures source-, section-, object-, and claim-level recall and utility; contradiction coverage; diversity and dependency; latency; and cost across query strata. Citation evaluation checks identity, location, fidelity, support strength, scope, and replay.

Protected evaluation queries and judgments are isolated from development and ordinary context. Metrics report uncertainty and failure slices rather than one aggregate score.

## 18.3 Reasoning and method evaluation

Intermediate artifacts are scored for question formulation, estimand or target, assumptions, evidence use, method fit, deterministic tool use, alternatives, leakage detection, uncertainty, and decision sensitivity. Expert disagreement is retained and adjudicated under a rubric.

Novelty claims require nearest-prior analysis. Calibration requires defined forecasts, outcomes, horizons, censoring, and scoring. Institutional learning evaluation asks whether applicable prior work and failure change subsequent tasks correctly, not whether the corpus grows.

> **Knowledge rule B3-KR18 — Evaluate the governed loop**  
> Acceptance evidence covers end-to-end retrieval, reasoning, contribution, permission, failure, and later reuse under identified versions; isolated model or index scores cannot establish Research Intelligence readiness.

## 18.4 Operational service levels

Operations monitor source freshness, catalog and object availability, queue and budget behavior, schema and citation consistency, permission enforcement, index lag, storage growth, backups, restore tests, and projection reconstruction. Service levels are defined by consequence and user task.

For scheduled literature surveillance, operations also monitor watchlist coverage, feed and credential health, expected-versus-observed issue arrivals, acquisition lag, duplicate rate, rights failures, recovery quality, triage backlog, alert precision, and time to governed disposition. A silent feed failure must be distinguishable from a week with no relevant publication. Periodic sampling measures whether agents missed material articles, overstated novelty, or routed techniques to the wrong owner.

Degraded behavior is explicit. If authoritative policy or protected evidence cannot be resolved, consequential decisions fail closed. Lower-risk retrieval can continue under a stale or partial indicator when policy permits.

## 18.5 Change and semantic equivalence

Parser, embedding, reranker, model, schema, or storage changes receive versioned regression evaluation. Rebuilt indexes need not produce byte-identical ranks; they must satisfy semantic and safety acceptance criteria and preserve historical replay where required.

Rollback includes data and projection compatibility, not merely application binaries. Changes that alter claim or lineage semantics require migration review and impact analysis.

## 18.6 Minimum accepted loop

An accepted initial capability can recover and cite a source, surface contradiction and gaps, retrieve applicable prior institutional work, preserve claim types, compile a replayable dossier, support a registration-ready hypothesis, ingest resulting experiment artifacts, record a scoped belief or decision update, and make the episode available to a later governed task. The scheduled-surveillance extension can poll an approved daily or weekly watchlist, prove coverage or expose acquisition failure, deduplicate and recover new publications, produce source-grounded technique briefs, and route each material item to an accountable disposition without treating it as adopted practice.

It need not autonomously create a valuable research program. It must demonstrate durable, attributable cognition across a complete loop and its failure paths.

## 18.7 Evolution

Research Intelligence evolves through evidence about its own performance. New cognitive services, graphs, and proactive discovery functions enter only after their dependencies and evaluation exist. It never gains production or governance authority through capability expansion.

Book X owns implementation phases. This volume defines what the system must preserve while every implementation component changes.
