# Chapter 3 — Knowledge Classes and Authority

One institutional library can contain doctrine, external science, internal experiments, and production records without treating them as interchangeable evidence. Research Intelligence uses four knowledge classes to preserve origin and authority while enabling cross-class retrieval.

## 3.1 Constitutional Knowledge

Constitutional Knowledge contains the Bible, adopted doctrine, controlled definitions, architectural decisions, and authoritative governance rules. It answers what the institution has committed to and which boundaries apply.

Its authority is institutional, not empirical. A constitutional statement can require trial registration; it cannot prove that a market relationship exists. Changes follow explicit canonical governance and retain prior versions.

## 3.2 Scientific Knowledge

Scientific Knowledge contains external papers, books, standards, datasets, mathematical results, methods, and domain evidence. It preserves editions, provenance, rights, extraction quality, retractions or corrections, and claim-level support.

External prestige is not universal authority. A primary paper may define its method precisely and still provide limited evidence for transfer to another market. A textbook may be authoritative for a standard derivation while not being the primary source of an empirical claim.

## 3.3 Institutional Knowledge

Institutional Knowledge contains questions, hypotheses, trials, experiments, failures, reviews, decisions, postmortems, beliefs, and lessons produced by Invariance Research. It answers what the institution attempted and learned.

Internal origin does not guarantee quality. Invalid runs, disputed interpretations, and rejected decisions remain typed. Primary laboratory artifacts carry different authority from a later summary.

## 3.4 Operational Knowledge

Operational Knowledge contains system documentation, ADRs, releases, configurations, runbooks, deployments, incidents, shadow and live records, reconciliations, and observed service behavior. It answers what systems were intended and observed to do.

Code and tests can establish implementation facts at a version. Production records can establish observed operation. Neither proves a scientific mechanism or future profitability.

> **Knowledge rule B3-KR03 — Authority follows the claim**  
> Retrieval and synthesis preserve knowledge class, source type, scope, provenance, and evidence relationship; no class or source has universal authority outside the claim it can support.

## 3.5 Cross-class reasoning

A promotion dossier can cite constitutional requirements, external statistical methods, internal experiments, and operational readiness evidence. The dossier does not flatten them into a relevance list. Each item states the claim it supports, its limitations, and dependencies.

Contradictions can occur within or across classes. An operational observation can contradict an implementation claim. An internal replication can weaken transfer of an external finding. A constitutional constraint can prohibit an otherwise scientifically interesting experiment.

## 3.6 Knowledge class is not memory form

Knowledge class describes origin and authority. Memory form describes cognitive use: semantic, episodic, procedural, working, executive, or reflective. A production incident is Operational Knowledge used as episodic memory; its runbook is Operational Knowledge used procedurally.

Keeping the axes separate resolves the competing hierarchies in the Founder’s Editions and allows access, retrieval, and reasoning policy to use the dimension that actually matters.
