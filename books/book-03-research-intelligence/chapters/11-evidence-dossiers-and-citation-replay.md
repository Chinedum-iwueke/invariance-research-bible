# Chapter 11 — Evidence Dossiers and Citation Replay

An evidence dossier is a decision-specific scientific briefing, not a search-results dump or a model essay. It assembles claims, primary evidence, contradictions, institutional history, uncertainty, and inference in a structure that an accountable reviewer can inspect and replay.

## 11.1 The dossier envelope

The stable envelope contains:

- decision, question, scope, and evidence cutoff;
- applicable doctrine and authority;
- normalized claims and their types;
- supporting, weakening, and contradictory evidence;
- methods, assumptions, populations, horizons, and dependencies;
- internal experiments, failures, decisions, and operational records;
- unknowns, access limitations, risks, and dissent;
- explicitly labeled synthesis and recommendation;
- retrieval and transformation manifest;
- compiler, reviewers, version, and expiry.

Domain extensions can add fields. Stability means a predictable envelope, not identical content for every decision.

## 11.2 Claim–evidence links

Each material sentence in the synthesis resolves to claim records and evidence objects. The relationship states whether the evidence directly supports, weakens, contextualizes, or merely cites the claim. Multiple summaries derived from one study retain their shared dependency.

Inference is explicit. A dossier can reason beyond source wording, but it states premises, method, alternatives, and uncertainty rather than attaching a citation that does not entail the conclusion.

> **Knowledge rule B3-KR11 — Every material claim is replayable**  
> A dossier permits a reviewer to navigate from synthesis to the exact source, experiment, or operational object and reconstruct the transformation and context by which that evidence entered the decision.

## 11.3 Citation validation

Citation validation checks identity, edition, location, quotation fidelity, local context, claim scope, and support strength. It distinguishes a source that contains related words from one that supports the asserted proposition.

Page, section, equation, table, figure, paragraph, or artifact coordinates are used as the source permits. Quotation limits and licenses govern how much source text appears in the dossier; paraphrase still requires faithful scope.

## 11.4 Replay

Replay reconstructs the dossier using recorded source and object versions, retrieval candidate set or snapshot, selected spans, transformations, and compiler configuration. It verifies historical decision context even when current indexes or summaries have improved.

A replay can expose that a source was later corrected, an artifact invalidated, or a policy superseded. The historical dossier remains intact and receives an impact annotation rather than being rewritten.

## 11.5 Review and expiry

Independent review examines omitted alternatives, source applicability, dependency, citation support, uncertainty, and decision sensitivity. Review comments and minority positions remain linked to the accepted dossier.

Dossiers expire when claims, policy, implementation, market conditions, or source status are time-sensitive. Expiry triggers refresh or an explicit decision to proceed with stale evidence under policy; it does not delete the prior record.

## 11.6 Minimal sufficiency

A dossier should be comprehensive enough for the decision and concise enough for substantive review. Appendices and expandable evidence preserve depth. Automated summarization cannot hide primary artifacts or adverse evidence behind an attractive executive view.

The dossier’s success is measured by decision traceability and evidence use, not document length.
