# Chapter 17 — Hermes Acceptance Criteria

Hermes exists only when a thin end-to-end governed loop works. Architecture diagrams, agent demonstrations, and policy prose are necessary evidence of design, not proof of operation. Acceptance requires exercised controls and reconstructible artifacts.

## 17.1 Minimum institutional loop

An accepted initial loop can:

1. resolve applicable doctrine and prior evidence;
2. register a question, hypothesis, and bounded trial;
3. authorize and execute a deterministic experiment;
4. preserve primary artifacts and search history;
5. perform independent evaluation from a frozen package;
6. issue an authenticated governance decision;
7. execute a prospective shadow observation under that decision;
8. close every branch, including invalid or rejected work;
9. publish artifacts, evidence, decision, and outcome to Research Intelligence;
10. retrieve the episode in a subsequent governed task.

Execution replay and demo can be later milestones if explicitly outside the initial boundary. Micro-live and production cannot be implied by shadow acceptance.

## 17.2 Control acceptance

Acceptance tests positive and negative paths: valid and invalid schemas, authorized and unauthorized roles, exhausted budgets, sealed-data denial, stale approvals, package substitution, duplicated requests, service outage, exception expiry, quarantine, rollback, and audit reconstruction.

The system must fail safely at consequential boundaries and degrade only where policy permits. An error message is not sufficient if a partial transition or untracked side effect remains.

## 17.3 Scientific and knowledge acceptance

The loop reproduces a declared result from identified inputs, distinguishes invalidity from a negative result, resolves citations to primary artifacts, surfaces an applicable contradiction or prior attempt, and preserves the decision-time dossier.

Acceptance does not require that memory “creates better questions,” which is not deterministically testable. It requires that the contribution is attributable, linked, retrievable, and available to the next task.

## 17.4 Operational acceptance

Identities, permissions, policy versions, service health, monitoring, alerts, reconciliation, backup, recovery, and incident paths are exercised in the accepted environment. Responsible humans understand the decision surfaces and can halt or revoke access.

Model and service changes have regression gates. Acceptance expires or is reevaluated when a material dependency, policy, schema, or risk boundary changes.

> **Operating rule B2-OR17 — Demonstrate the loop**  
> Hermes acceptance is established by an exercised, auditable end-to-end loop and its failure paths, not by component availability or a favorable research result.

## 17.5 Evidence of acceptance

The acceptance dossier contains scope, versions, test plan, raw results, defects, exceptions, residual risks, accountable approval, expiry, and excluded capabilities. Statements about Hermes remain proposals until this evidence exists for an identified implementation.
