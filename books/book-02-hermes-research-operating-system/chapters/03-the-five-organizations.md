# Chapter 3 — The Five Organizations

Hermes coordinates five organizations: Research, Engineering, Knowledge, Governance, and Production. They are domains of accountability, not necessarily five legal entities, buildings, or fixed headcounts. Their boundaries persist even when one person temporarily holds multiple low-risk roles or an agent assists several teams.

The structure supersedes the Founder’s Edition’s seven-division model. Responsibilities are mapped into clearer authority domains rather than discarded.

## 3.1 Research

Research owns scientific questions, hypotheses, mechanisms, representations, experiment proposals, interpretation, and research-program direction. It explains why a claim should be investigated and what evidence would change the institution’s understanding.

Research may propose candidates and criticize results. It does not approve its own promotion, grant itself sealed evidence, change risk limits, or operate production capital. The Senior Quantitative Researcher is a synthesis and direction role within this organization, not a universal authority.

## 3.2 Engineering

Engineering owns deterministic platforms, data contracts, software quality, service reliability, identity integration, deployment mechanisms, and technical controls. It makes experiments and operations reproducible, observable, and safe.

Engineering can declare an artifact technically invalid or a system unready. It cannot infer scientific support from passing tests or authorize capital because software is reliable. Its ownership of implementation does not include unilateral authority to change the scientific contract.

## 3.3 Knowledge

Knowledge operates Research Intelligence: source stewardship, provenance, identity resolution, retrieval, evidence assembly, belief lineage, graph projections, curation, and retention. It ensures that work can find applicable prior evidence and that material outcomes return to institutional memory.

Knowledge distinguishes librarianship from canonical judgment. Retrieval quality and metadata do not transform weak evidence into strong evidence. The Canon Curator’s decisions follow controlled authority and remain reviewable.

## 3.4 Governance

Governance owns institutional policy, decision procedures, trial and exception controls, independent evaluation requirements, security authorization, audit, and the integrity of promotion and demotion. It protects the separation between those who propose, test, validate, and authorize.

Governance does not become a research collaborator or portfolio optimizer. It evaluates whether required process and evidence exist, applies vetoes within scope, and records decisions. Policy itself is versioned, appealable, auditable, and subject to controlled change.

## 3.5 Production

Production owns capital-bearing operation, operational readiness, venue connectivity, reconciliation, monitoring, incident containment, and execution within approved mandates and risk budgets. It can halt or reduce exposure under delegated safety authority.

Production does not reinterpret a hypothesis to justify an outcome or promote an unapproved candidate. Observed production behavior returns as evidence rather than becoming an alternative scientific record.

## 3.6 Shared decisions

Material decisions cross organizations. Portfolio admission may require Research’s claim and limitations, Engineering’s reproducibility and readiness evidence, Knowledge’s dossier and lineage, Governance’s conformance decision, and Production’s operational assessment. One accountable decision owner is named for the bounded decision, while independent vetoes remain explicit.

Shared contribution does not mean consensus authority. A RACI-style mapping can clarify who is responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed, but it cannot erase statutory, risk, security, or scientific vetoes. Chapter 4 formalizes these rights.

> **Operating rule B2-OR03 — Organizational boundary**  
> Every material capability and decision has a named organizational home, explicit interfaces, and stated vetoes; collaboration does not dissolve accountability.

## 3.7 Small-team operation

Early in the institution’s life, individuals may occupy several roles. Separation is then enforced through time, artifacts, permissions, and independent review rather than payroll titles. A researcher can implement exploratory code but cannot alone validate and promote the resulting candidate. An engineer can operate a test environment but cannot grant production eligibility.

Where independence cannot be achieved internally, the work remains at a lower maturity, receives external review, or is deferred. Organizational aspiration cannot be used as evidence that a control exists.

## 3.8 Capability mapping

Former quantitative research, machine learning, and market-structure divisions map principally to Research, with specialist engineering and production interfaces. Data and platform map to Engineering. Memory and curation map to Knowledge. Risk and governance responsibilities divide between Governance’s policy and admission authority and Production’s live control, with portfolio science owned as defined in Book VII.

This mapping prevents a second organizational taxonomy from competing with the canonical five. Specialist disciplines remain visible as capabilities, chapters, and roles without becoming independent authority trees.
