# Chapter 6 — Programs, Questions, and Bounded Autonomy

Research requires freedom to follow evidence, but freedom without scope can consume protected data, compute, attention, and institutional credibility without a stopping condition. Hermes delegates exploration through research programs and bounded task charters.

## 6.1 Research programs

A research program groups related questions around a scientific objective, domain, or institutional need. Its charter records:

- purpose, scope, and decision relevance;
- accountable program owner and participating organizations;
- eligible markets, data, representations, methods, and tools;
- trial, compute, elapsed-time, and human-attention budgets;
- protected evidence and access rules;
- required reviews, outputs, and reporting cadence;
- stopping, renewal, suspension, and closeout conditions.

Programs can contain exploratory, confirmatory, replication, infrastructure, and negative-control work. They are portfolios of inquiry, not containers for strategies already presumed valuable.

## 6.2 Questions enter through a controlled agenda

A question enters with provenance: anomaly, contradiction, prior result, operational need, external source, or deliberate exploration. It states scope and relationship to existing work. Research Intelligence supplies relevant prior attempts and unresolved contradictions before material resources are committed.

The agenda preserves questions that are deferred or rejected and the reason. A ranking is decision support, not objective truth. Expected information gain, feasibility, urgency, novelty, mechanism importance, risk, and portfolio relevance may inform allocation, but no single score acquires budget authority.

## 6.3 Task charters

A task charter instantiates a bounded portion of the program. It identifies inputs, permitted actions, prohibited actions, tool and data scopes, budgets, output contract, stopping rules, and escalation conditions. Autonomous workers may choose tactics within the charter; they may not broaden the scientific question or access boundary by inference.

> **Operating rule B2-OR06 — Autonomy ends at the charter**  
> A worker may adapt methods within an authorized task but must stop, close, or request amendment when the next useful action exceeds scope, budget, evidence access, or decision authority.

Amendments preserve the original charter, evidence already observed, reason for change, approver, and revised budget. Material post-result expansion creates a new trial family or question when necessary to protect interpretation.

## 6.4 Budgets and stopping

Budgets cover more than compute. They include trial count, search breadth, holdout exposure, data licensing, external calls, elapsed time, operational disruption, and reviewer attention. Exhaustion causes a deterministic stop or governed request; the worker cannot hide attempts to remain within a count.

Stopping can follow resolved uncertainty, futility, invalid data, budget exhaustion, safety concern, supersession, or lack of decision relevance. A stopped program receives a closeout artifact. “More testing may help” is not sufficient reason for endless continuation.

## 6.5 Portfolio oversight

Governance approves the charter and enforces limits. Research directs scientific content. Knowledge supplies prior evidence and records outcomes. Engineering controls technical resources. Production participates when prospective or operational environments are involved.

Program review examines what was learned, search exposure, resource use, unresolved risks, and whether continuation remains more valuable than alternatives. It does not reward candidate count or favorable results alone.
