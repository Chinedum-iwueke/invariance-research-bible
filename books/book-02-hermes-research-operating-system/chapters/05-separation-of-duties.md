# Chapter 5 — Separation of Duties

Scientific and operational failures become more likely when one actor can propose a claim, choose its test, inspect protected evidence, judge the result, approve promotion, and operate capital. Hermes separates these duties so that enthusiasm, error, or compromise at one point cannot silently control the entire path.

## 5.1 The separated capabilities

The core sequence distinguishes:

1. **originating** a question, hypothesis, or change;
2. **specifying** the experiment or implementation;
3. **executing** the registered work;
4. **evaluating** evidence independently;
5. **authorizing** maturity, environment, or policy change;
6. **operating** the approved capability;
7. **auditing** the process and records.

These are capabilities, not necessarily permanent job titles. The required degree of separation follows consequence, reversibility, sealed-data exposure, and conflict of interest.

> **Operating rule B2-OR05 — No self-promotion**  
> No actor or materially dependent group may originate, evaluate, and authorize the same consequential promotion without an explicit, time-bounded exception and compensating independent control.

## 5.2 Meaningful independence

Different names do not create independence. Two agents can share a model, prompt lineage, retrieved context, code, evaluator, and incentives. Two humans can report to the same outcome owner or collaborate on the same analysis. A second run through the same defective pipeline is reproduction, not independent validation.

An independence assessment records shared dependencies in data, representations, implementation, models, evidence access, incentives, and authority. Perfect independence is rarely possible; the institution states remaining dependence and adjusts the strength of the review claim.

Validators receive a frozen evidence package so the object under review cannot change silently. They may request additions through an attributable channel. The request and response create a new package version; the originator cannot replace unfavorable evidence inside the reviewed version.

## 5.3 Permission enforcement

Separation is enforced through capabilities scoped to identity, resource, action, environment, time, and purpose. A researcher may register a trial without permission to expose the holdout. An executor may read an authorized dataset without changing the registration. A validator may inspect results without issuing the promotion transition.

Where a small team requires one person to perform multiple duties, temporal separation, immutable artifacts, external review, or restricted maturity can compensate. The limitation remains visible. The institution does not claim a control merely because its future organization chart contains separate boxes.

## 5.4 Operational and emergency separation

Production operators can halt, isolate, or reduce risk under delegated authority. They cannot expand an approved mandate during an incident. Engineering can repair a service but cannot deploy an unreviewed semantic change into a capital-bearing path. Governance can waive an operating control only within its exception authority and cannot waive constitutional boundaries.

Break-glass access requires strong identity, reason, narrow scope, expiry, alerting, and retrospective review. Credentials and protected content are not copied into general logs.

## 5.5 Assurance

Assurance attempts prohibited combinations: originator promotion, validator mutation, expired-role execution, unreviewed package substitution, and production operation after revocation. It also tests that legitimate escalation and emergency containment remain possible.

Separation is not bureaucratic delay for its own sake. It is an error-control architecture. Its cost should be proportionate, but its absence must never be disguised as agility.
