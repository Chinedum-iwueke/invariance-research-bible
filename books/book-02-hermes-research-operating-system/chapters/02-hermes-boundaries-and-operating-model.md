# Chapter 2 — Hermes Boundaries and Operating Model

Invariance Research is the institution. Hermes is its governed operating system and organization: the coordinated structure of roles, policies, workflows, permissions, services, artifacts, and decisions by which research becomes controlled action. Treating Hermes as a model, chatbot, or orchestration framework would place the durable institution inside a replaceable component.

## 2.1 What Hermes owns

Hermes owns organizational coordination and control. Its domain includes:

- role and authority assignment;
- program charters and bounded research work;
- lifecycle and state-transition policy;
- registration, review, decision, exception, and incident workflows;
- permission requests and enforcement interfaces;
- budgets, stopping conditions, expiry, and escalation;
- evidence requirements for promotion and demotion;
- the obligation to publish material outcomes to Research Intelligence.

Ownership means authority over the operating contract, not implementation of every specialized capability. Hermes requests laboratory work from Bulletproof BT, knowledge services from Research Intelligence, portfolio analysis from the relevant organization, and execution behavior from production systems.

## 2.2 What Hermes does not own

Hermes is not the canonical corpus, graph, retrieval engine, or durable memory; those belong to Research Intelligence. It is not the backtester or execution simulator; Bulletproof BT is the deterministic laboratory. It is not the portfolio allocator, venue adapter, model runtime, data warehouse, or deployment topology.

Nor does Hermes become the scientific authority that declares claims true. It coordinates evaluation and records decisions. Epistemic assessments remain evidence-linked objects; accountable bodies exercise the decision rights defined in this book.

This boundary prevents a central operating layer from becoming an unreviewable super-system.

> **Operating rule B2-OR02 — Coordination without absorption**  
> Hermes governs how specialist capabilities are requested, evidenced, authorized, and reviewed without becoming the authoritative implementation of those capabilities.

## 2.3 Durable capabilities, replaceable workers

Hermes names durable capabilities such as research direction, experiment execution, independent validation, knowledge curation, policy enforcement, portfolio admission, risk review, and production operation. A capability can be performed by a human, agent, deterministic service, or governed combination.

Workers are bound to task instances. Each instance has an identity, role, charter, permitted tools, input artifacts, resource budget, output contract, and terminal disposition. Replacing the worker does not change the decision right. Giving two workers different names does not establish independence when they share evidence, model lineage, code, incentives, or authority.

## 2.4 Control plane and work plane

The operating model distinguishes a control plane from a work plane. The control plane evaluates identity, policy, budgets, state, required evidence, and permitted transitions. The work plane performs research, computation, retrieval, validation, and operational activity within the resulting authorization.

This is a logical boundary, not a mandated deployment topology. Services may share infrastructure when risk permits, but control decisions remain explicit and auditable. A work-plane component cannot broaden its own permissions or declare its output approved.

Capital-bearing and security-sensitive paths fail closed when their required control plane is unavailable. Exploratory paths may degrade safely under policy, but the degradation is visible and cannot access sealed evidence or production authority.

## 2.5 Canonical state and projections

Hermes stores or references canonical identities and transition records. Dashboards, task graphs, queues, and combined lifecycle views are projections. They may summarize state but cannot silently become independent sources of truth.

The operating model preserves four orthogonal dimensions defined by ADR-0004: epistemic state, candidate maturity, validation environment, and operational state. A shadow run does not make a candidate mature. A supported claim does not make a service ready. A running production service does not prove realizable alpha.

## 2.6 Interfaces among the three central systems

Hermes sends a governed experimental request to Bulletproof BT with a registered specification, authorized data identities, environment fingerprint, and budget. Bulletproof BT returns immutable run and evidence artifacts. Hermes routes those artifacts to independent review and records any decision.

Hermes requests evidence dossiers and prior-work retrieval from Research Intelligence. It publishes new artifacts and decisions back through controlled contribution interfaces. Research Intelligence can surface contradictions or applicable policy, but it cannot execute a promotion transition.

Model runtimes interact through bounded cognitive services. Their outputs retain model, prompt or instruction, tool, retrieval, and context provenance at the level required by consequence and retention policy.

## 2.7 Non-goals

Hermes does not seek unrestricted autonomy, universal centralization, replacement of accountable humans, or optimization of institutional activity by a single score. It does not guarantee correct decisions. It does not treat workflow completion as scientific success.

The target is a legible institution: work enters through explicit authority, travels through typed interfaces, encounters independent review, changes state through deterministic controls, and returns its outcome to durable memory.
