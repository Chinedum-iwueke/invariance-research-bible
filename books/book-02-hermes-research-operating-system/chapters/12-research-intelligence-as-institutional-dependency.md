# Chapter 12 — Research Intelligence as Institutional Dependency

Hermes cannot govern reliable research without access to institutional memory. Before a material decision, it must be able to recover applicable doctrine, prior work, contradictory evidence, implementation history, and unresolved risk. After the decision, it must return the resulting artifacts and reasoning.

Research Intelligence supplies that dependency. Book III owns its internals; Book II defines the service obligations that governed operation requires.

## 12.1 Evidence retrieval

A Hermes task can request a scoped evidence dossier containing relevant claims, sources, experiments, failures, contradictions, beliefs, operational records, and limitations. The request identifies the question, decision, time boundary, authority, protected-content eligibility, and required provenance.

The dossier distinguishes primary evidence from summaries and states retrieval coverage and known omissions. It cannot certify that no relevant evidence exists. High-consequence decisions may require targeted opposition search, specialist review, or a controlled benchmark beyond ordinary retrieval.

## 12.2 Canonical contribution

Hermes publishes questions, registrations, run references, reviews, decisions, exceptions, incidents, postmortems, and state transitions through typed contribution interfaces. Research Intelligence validates identity, lineage, schema, access, and retention metadata before incorporation.

Contribution does not make content canonical. The Librarian can remediate metadata; the Canon Curator can assign controlled status within authority; neither changes evidence strength by classification alone.

> **Operating rule B2-OR12 — Retrieve before, return after**  
> Every material governed task retrieves applicable institutional evidence before decision and returns its attributable outcome, including failure or non-change, to Research Intelligence at closeout.

Exceptions apply only where urgency or availability policy permits. The task records the missing retrieval and requires later reconciliation.

## 12.3 Belief and decision boundaries

Research Intelligence preserves versioned epistemic assessments. Hermes records which assessment and evidence dossier a decision used. A governance decision can authorize action under uncertainty without rewriting the belief; new evidence can revise a belief without granting operational permission.

This boundary preserves ADR-0004’s state separation. Research Intelligence does not promote candidates or allocate capital. Hermes does not manufacture epistemic state from workflow outcomes.

## 12.4 Availability and degraded operation

The dependency has consequence-based service levels. If authoritative policy or decision evidence cannot be resolved, a promotion transition fails closed. A low-risk exploratory task may continue under a degraded-mode policy using cached public knowledge, but it cannot claim complete institutional review.

Cached objects include identity, version, effective interval, and freshness. Recovery reconciles queued contributions and detects conflicts rather than overwriting whichever copy arrived last.

## 12.5 Access and minimization

Retrieval respects source licensing, confidentiality, sealed holdouts, personal data, regional limits, and purpose. A derived index inherits restrictions. Agent-facing context contains only the minimum needed for the task, and contribution filters prevent credentials or prohibited content from entering durable memory.

Access denial is evidence about authority, not proof that the hidden content supports or contradicts the claim. Decisions record the limitation.

## 12.6 Service assurance

Assurance tests source replay, citation resolution, provenance completeness, applicable-prior-work retrieval, contradiction surfacing, access denial, contribution lineage, supersession, and retention behavior. Retrieval metrics require versioned benchmarks, relevance judgments, query strata, and uncertainty; a universal recall threshold is not constitutional.

The institutional dependency succeeds when a decision can discover what the organization already knows and when its outcome changes the memory available to the next governed task.
