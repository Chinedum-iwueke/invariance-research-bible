# Chapter 16 — Operating Cadence and Institutional Review

Different institutional questions require different clocks. Fast observation and containment must coexist with slower scientific, portfolio, policy, and architectural review. Hermes defines cadence so urgency does not inherit authority over decisions that require deeper evidence.

## 16.1 Event-driven operation

Incidents, hard risk breaches, security events, data-integrity failures, and material model deviations trigger immediate workflows. Event-driven action follows pre-authorized containment and escalation rules. It does not wait for a calendar meeting.

## 16.2 Daily review

Daily operation reviews system health, active experiments, protected evidence use, shadow and production observations, limit events, unresolved failures, and required handoffs. Its purpose is situational awareness and safe disposition, not daily strategy reinvention.

## 16.3 Weekly and program review

Weekly or program-specific review examines questions, search budgets, experiment validity, contradiction, resource use, and near-term decisions. It can close, amend, or prioritize work within program authority.

## 16.4 Monthly and quarterly review

Slower review examines candidate maturity, portfolio fit, model and cost stability, incidents, policy exceptions, retrieval and memory quality, agent evaluation, capacity, and strategic research balance. Architectural and constitutional changes follow their own adoption process rather than calendar pressure.

> **Operating rule B2-OR16 — Cadence cannot confer authority**  
> A meeting, deadline, or market event may trigger a decision workflow but does not reduce its evidence, separation, or authorization requirements except through an explicit emergency policy.

## 16.5 Review artifacts

Every review has scope, participants, decision rights, evidence cutoff, agenda, decisions, dissent, actions, owners, deadlines, and next review. Dashboards link canonical metrics and state rather than becoming an alternative record.

Stale or missing evidence is visible. A review can defer a decision; silence does not create approval. Actions remain open until verified or explicitly closed with reason.

## 16.6 Institutional meta-review

The institution reviews whether its cadence produces learning and control. Warning signs include repeated rediscovery, unresolved actions, ceremonial approval, exceptions without expiry, validators overloaded by low-value work, or fast committees routinely overriding slower evidence.

Cadence is adjusted through policy with preserved history. The goal is neither maximum meeting frequency nor continuous automated motion. It is timely, accountable attention at the clock appropriate to the risk and claim.
