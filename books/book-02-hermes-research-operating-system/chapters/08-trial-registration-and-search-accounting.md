# Chapter 8 — Trial Registration and Search Accounting

Adaptive research changes what later evidence means. When specifications, parameters, samples, and interpretations respond to observed results, the unreported alternatives remain part of the evidential context. Hermes preserves that context through registration and search accounting.

## 8.1 Registration before privileged evaluation

A trial registration identifies the question, hypothesis, mechanism or predictive rationale, alternatives, representation, eligible data, design, metrics, exclusions, experiment family, search budget, stopping rules, and holdout policy. It receives an immutable identity before the relevant privileged evaluation begins.

Registration is not a claim that the plan is optimal. It establishes what was committed before the evidence and makes later adaptation legible.

## 8.2 Units of search

No single counter captures research multiplicity. Hermes records:

- hypothesis creation and material revision;
- experiment specifications and variants;
- parameter searches and selection rules;
- dataset, universe, horizon, and representation choices;
- cheap screens and terminated runs;
- metric and result views;
- holdout or sealed-evidence exposure;
- post-result interpretation and amendment;
- candidate-family and shared-dependency relationships.

These events form search history. Counts do not automatically determine a statistical correction because tests can be dependent and selection mechanisms differ. Book IV chooses methods appropriate to the design.

> **Operating rule B2-OR08 — Attempts remain visible**  
> Every material attempt and privileged evidence exposure is appended to search history with lineage; retry, failure, or amendment never overwrites the event.

## 8.3 Protected evidence

Protected evidence has an owner, purpose, access policy, eligible roles, exposure budget, and release conditions. A holdout is not protected merely because it sits in a different file. Summary statistics, charts, rankings, and model feedback can leak its information.

Access is mediated and logged. The returned result is minimized to the authorized purpose where possible. Exhausted evidence cannot regain independence by being renamed or copied into a new dataset version.

## 8.4 Amendments and exploratory work

Unexpected findings and defects legitimately change research. An amendment records the prior registration, evidence already observed, change, reason, author, approval if required, and interpretive consequence. Major changes create a new registered trial or family.

Exploratory work is allowed and labeled. Its result can generate a new confirmatory design but cannot be presented as if independently predicted. Hermes values discovery without laundering exploration into confirmation.

## 8.5 The Research Governor

The Research Governor enforces registration presence, budgets, protected-data rules, stopping conditions, and required closeout. It is a governance capability, not a scientist or collaborator. It does not choose hypotheses, adjust results, or decide that evidence is compelling.

Governor policy is versioned and auditable. Exceptions have expiry and review. An unavailable Governor fails closed for sealed evidence and other consequential controls; low-risk open exploration may continue only under an adopted degraded-mode policy.

## 8.6 Closeout and assurance

Closeout records completed, invalid, interrupted, rejected, superseded, or unresolved status; used resources; exposed evidence; produced artifacts; and resulting questions or decisions. Invalid trials preserve engineering lessons without becoming scientific nulls.

Assurance tests registration timing, immutable history, duplicate and replay behavior, exposure accounting, budget enforcement, amendment lineage, and denial of unauthorized access. Search accounting succeeds when a reviewer can reconstruct how the reported result emerged—not merely when a registry row exists.
