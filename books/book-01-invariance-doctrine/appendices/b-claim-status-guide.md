# Appendix B — Claim-Status Guide

Use a label when a reasonable reader could confuse a commitment, implemented behavior, observation, design intention, or interpretation.

| Status | Use | Required basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Doctrine** | Controlled institutional commitment | Canonical adoption and authority |
| **Implementation fact** | Behavior present at an identified version | Code, schema, test, documentation, or observed artifact |
| **Empirical result** | Observation produced by a stated method | Data, method, uncertainty, provenance, and scope |
| **Proposal** | Target design or behavior not yet adopted or implemented | Rationale, owner, status, and decision path |
| **Inference** | Reasoned conclusion from stated premises | Premises, logic, alternatives, and uncertainty |

Absence of a label never permits ambiguity. A chapter may explain an adopted definition without repeating **Doctrine** on every sentence, but it must not describe a proposal as implemented or an implementation fact as empirical validation.

Citation authority remains claim-relative. Source type, prestige, and recency do not replace verification that the cited material supports the specific sentence.
