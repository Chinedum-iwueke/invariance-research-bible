# Chapter 6 — Questions, Anomalies, and Discovery

Optimization begins inside a defined space. It searches parameters, rules, allocations, or policies for an objective. Discovery can alter the space itself. It asks whether the target is meaningful, whether the representation exposes the phenomenon, whether an unexplained residual deserves attention, and whether the institution has been solving the wrong problem.

Invariance Research therefore places scientific questions and anomalies before performance optimization. This ordering does not prohibit optimization. It prevents optimization from manufacturing the question after the result is visible.

## 6.1 The question defines the investigation

A serious research question identifies more than a topic. It defines the object, scope, uncertainty, and decision that motivate the inquiry. “Does momentum work?” is not yet a research question. It leaves momentum, universe, horizon, availability, benchmark, cost, state, and intended use undefined.

A bounded question might ask whether a causally available measure of cross-sectional persistence changes the distribution of future residual returns over specified horizons among liquid perpetual contracts, after stated costs, and whether the relationship contributes information beyond existing candidates. The formulation can still be wrong, but it exposes what evidence and alternatives matter.

Questions shape representations, data requirements, estimands, methods, experiments, and stopping rules. They also constrain interpretation. A result about one residual definition and horizon does not answer every question called momentum.

> **Doctrine B1-PR05 — Question first**  
> The institution defines the scientific question, scope, and decision relevance before selecting the optimization objective or evaluating privileged evidence.

## 6.2 Anomalies are not opportunities

An anomaly is a scoped deviation from an explicit expectation. The expectation can come from a model, mechanism, benchmark, prior distribution, operational contract, or established institutional belief. Without the expectation, surprise is merely attention.

An anomaly record states the observation, expected behavior, effect magnitude, uncertainty, data availability, search context, and plausible artifact explanations. It may concern returns, residuals, liquidity, funding, execution, risk, infrastructure, or contradiction between sources. It may be persistent or transient. It may never become tradable.

The distinction is constitutional. A rare event does not become an opportunity because it is unusual. A detector score does not become a signal. The institution may dismiss, monitor, investigate, or use the anomaly to register a question. Hypothesis formation and trading require later authority and evidence.

## 6.3 Search creates its own evidence problem

The more flexible the search, the easier it becomes to select an apparently exceptional result. The risk is not limited to deliberate data mining. Analysis choices can depend on observed data even when each choice appears theoretically defensible. Gelman and Loken describe this adaptive multiplicity as a “garden of forking paths,” in which the unexecuted alternatives still matter to interpretation ([Gelman and Loken, 2013](../../../bibliography/references.md#gelman-loken-2013)).

Ioannidis’s analytical argument likewise shows how low prior odds, small effects, flexible designs, bias, and many tested relationships can reduce the credibility of claimed findings ([Ioannidis, 2005](../../../bibliography/references.md#ioannidis-2005)). The result arises from a particular modeling framework and is not a theorem that every field or market claim is false. Its relevance here is methodological: search breadth and selection belong to the evidence, not merely to the researcher’s private process.

The Trial Registry therefore records families, candidates, specifications, parameter variations, cheap screens, failed runs, result views, holdout exposures, and post-result adaptations. Counting attempts does not by itself produce a correct statistical adjustment. It makes the actual adaptive process available to the methods that need to account for it.

## 6.4 Discovery before optimization

The doctrine imposes a sequence:

1. observe a phenomenon, contradiction, residual, or knowledge gap;
2. formulate a scoped scientific question;
3. retrieve prior evidence, failures, and alternative explanations;
4. propose a mechanism or predictive rationale and competing accounts;
5. justify the representation and target;
6. register the hypothesis, search family, evidence plan, and stopping rules;
7. execute and criticize the experiment;
8. optimize implementation only after the underlying relationship has earned support appropriate to the decision.

The sequence is a default, not a claim that every engineering optimization needs a market hypothesis. Safety, reliability, numerical efficiency, and operational cost can be optimized as engineering objectives. The prohibition concerns using strategy optimization to create retrospective scientific meaning.

Optimization becomes valuable once the object is stable enough to justify it. Parameter selection, execution design, portfolio allocation, and model tuning can improve realization. Their search remains registered and their uncertainty remains visible.

## 6.5 Evidence is broader than significance

Discovery cannot be reduced to a threshold. The American Statistical Association’s statement on p-values emphasizes that a p-value does not measure the probability that a hypothesis is true or the importance of a result, and that scientific conclusions should not be based only on whether a threshold is crossed ([Wasserstein and Lazar, 2016](../../../bibliography/references.md#wasserstein-lazar-2016)).

The institution evaluates effect magnitude, uncertainty, dependence, data quality, search history, stability, cost, scope, alternatives, and decision consequence. The relevant design may be frequentist, Bayesian, predictive, causal, simulation-based, or primarily operational. No single statistic governs every claim.

A broad plateau across nearby specifications can be reassuring when the mechanism implies smoothness. A narrow effect can be genuine when market rules create a threshold. Cross-market replication can strengthen a market-general claim while being irrelevant to a venue-specific mechanism. Robustness is always robustness to specified perturbations.

## 6.6 Discovery portfolios

The institution cannot investigate every question. Attention, protected data access, time, compute, engineering capacity, and capital are constrained. Research programs therefore form a portfolio of incremental, strategic, exploratory, validation, and infrastructure work.

Expected information gain can help express the value of resolving uncertainty, but it is not an oracle. The estimate depends on beliefs about outcomes, consequences, feasibility, and cost. Novelty, urgency, portfolio relevance, mechanism importance, and risk also matter. Governance allocates resources; Research Intelligence supplies evidence and unresolved questions; neither an Opportunity Graph nor an agent acquires autonomous budget authority.

Diversity has value. A program concentrated in one representation, mechanism, venue, horizon, or method can produce many candidates while learning one thing repeatedly. The institution seeks diversity of questions and explanations without treating variety as an objective independent of quality.

## 6.7 Bounded autonomous research

Autonomous search is permitted inside a charter. The charter states the scientific objective, data and holdout access, markets, representations, methods, tools, budgets, elapsed time, stopping rules, required artifacts, and forbidden actions. Workers may be creative within it. They cannot expand the charter, conceal attempts, or promote their own findings.

Every session has a terminal disposition. It may register a candidate, request more evidence, close for futility, report invalid data, defer for cost, or preserve an unresolved question. A system that never stops can always rationalize another test; stopping is part of scientific governance.

Book IV develops the discovery laboratory, including search languages, mechanisms, specialized roles, graphs, selection correction, and adversarial review. Book II owns authority and budgets. Book III preserves the evidence and search history. Book I fixes the order among them.

## 6.8 Negative discovery

Discovery includes learning that an anomaly was a timestamp artifact, that a celebrated relationship was driven by one period, that a model added no information beyond a baseline, or that current evidence cannot distinguish among explanations. These outcomes reduce uncertainty or prevent future waste when the work is valid and remembered.

The institution does not reward negativity as a pose. A weak design cannot earn scientific value by failing. Nor does it require every experiment to overturn a belief. The contribution is the justified change in the evidence map: a claim weakened, a boundary identified, an artifact diagnosed, or a question sharpened.

## 6.9 The discovery engine

Discovery is not a single engine, graph, model, or agent. It is the governed interaction of observation, representation, questioning, retrieval, hypothesis, experiment, criticism, prospective evidence, decision, and memory. The loop becomes more capable when earlier episodes improve later formulation and when failures become easier to detect.

Its success cannot be measured by candidate volume or backtest performance alone. Useful measures include avoided duplication, time to falsification, retrieval of applicable failures, validity of registered tests, calibration of claims, and the quality of decisions under uncertainty. Even these can be gamed if detached from their purpose.

The durable objective is not an endless stream of ideas. It is an institution that chooses better questions, exposes them to stronger tests, and records the result with enough integrity that the next question begins from a more reliable place.
