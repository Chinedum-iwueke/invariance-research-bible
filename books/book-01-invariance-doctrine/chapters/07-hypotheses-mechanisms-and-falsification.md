# Chapter 7 — Hypotheses, Mechanisms, and Falsification

A research idea can be suggestive without being testable. A model can be useful without expressing a scientific claim. A profitable configuration can occur without revealing why it worked. Invariance Research uses the hypothesis to convert curiosity into a claim that can encounter evidence, criticism, and failure.

The hypothesis is not sacred because it is formal. Its value lies in the commitments it makes before privileged evidence is seen: what is expected, where, when, by how much, under which representation, against which alternatives, and what observations would weaken the claim. Registration makes those commitments inspectable. It does not make them correct.

## 7.1 The hypothesis as a scientific object

A hypothesis states a bounded relationship or mechanism and the conditions under which the institution expects to observe its consequences. It identifies the population or market domain, information set, decision time, outcome horizon, representation, expected direction or structure, and relevant uncertainty. Where a point prediction is unjustified, it can state an ordering, dependence, invariance, boundary, or qualitative discriminating prediction.

“Liquidity predicts returns” is too loose. A testable claim identifies what liquidity means, when it is observed, which future outcome is addressed, which confounders and baselines matter, and which result would count against the proposed account. Precision does not require false certainty; it requires exposing ambiguity before ambiguity can protect a favored result.

The hypothesis is versioned. Material changes to the target, sample, horizon, representation, exclusions, metric, or interpretation create a new version or a documented amendment. The earlier commitment remains visible. This prevents the final statement from being silently rewritten to fit the result.

> **Doctrine B1-PR06 — Exposed claims**  
> A scientific claim must expose its scope, assumptions, alternatives, and failure conditions before it can earn institutional confidence.

## 7.2 Mechanisms discipline explanation

A mechanism describes a process through which the proposed relationship could arise. In markets, it may concern incentives, constraints, information acquisition, inventory, forced flow, institutional rules, attention, risk transfer, or participant adaptation. A predictive rationale can be weaker than a fully identified causal mechanism, but it must still explain why the information should precede the outcome and why the relationship may persist long enough to matter.

Mechanisms serve three purposes. They generate discriminating predictions beyond the initial pattern. They identify boundary conditions under which an effect should weaken or reverse. They organize competing explanations so that evidence can distinguish among them.

A plausible story is not evidence that the mechanism operates. Many narratives fit the same return series after the fact. Conversely, the absence of an identified causal mechanism does not automatically prohibit a bounded predictive or risk-control use. The institution labels the claim correctly: predictive evidence supports prediction; explanatory evidence supports an account; causal evidence requires an identification strategy appropriate to the intervention or counterfactual claimed.

## 7.3 Nulls and alternatives

An experiment becomes informative when it compares the focal account with credible alternatives. A statistical null may express no effect under a specified model, but it is not the only alternative. Apparent predictability may arise from leakage, stale prices, survivorship, benchmark exposure, data revisions, search selection, one market episode, cost omission, or an existing signal in different notation.

The registered hypothesis therefore includes an alternative set. Some alternatives are scientific, some statistical, and some operational. Their implications determine diagnostic tests and robustness checks. A design that can reject only a convenient zero-effect model while ignoring the most plausible artifact is not severe merely because it reports a small p-value.

Failure to reject a null does not establish that the focal effect is exactly absent. Rejection does not establish the preferred mechanism. Both conclusions depend on design, power, assumptions, multiplicity, and the range of alternatives considered.

## 7.4 What falsification can and cannot mean

Falsification is an institutional commitment to let adverse evidence change a claim. It is not a mechanical rule by which one surprising observation destroys every theory. Measurements can fail, auxiliary assumptions can be wrong, and stochastic hypotheses rarely imply a single deterministic outcome.

The registered plan should therefore specify failure conditions at the appropriate level. A candidate may fail because the estimated effect is economically negligible, uncertainty is too large, a discriminating prediction reverses, the effect vanishes under a required cost model, or prospective evidence contradicts the fitted relationship. A mechanism may be weakened even while prediction remains useful. An implementation may fail while the scientific claim remains unresolved.

The institution records which object encountered failure. It does not translate every adverse result into a universal refutation or every favorable result into confirmation.

## 7.5 Registration and adaptation

Registration preserves the state of the inquiry before evaluation. It includes the question, hypothesis, mechanism, alternatives, representation, data eligibility, experiment family, metrics, exclusions, search budget, stopping rules, and holdout policy. Book II governs the record; Books IV and V implement research and laboratory contracts.

Registration does not prohibit learning during analysis. Unexpected defects require repair; anomalies can justify new questions; an exploratory result can motivate a new confirmatory trial. The rule is separation and disclosure. An amendment records what changed, when, why, and which evidence had already been observed. A post-result hypothesis may be valuable, but it begins with less independent evidence than one stated in advance.

Protected evidence is a finite institutional resource. Repeated inspection turns a holdout into training information even if no row is copied into a fitting set. Access therefore follows explicit policy, and exposure becomes part of search history.

## 7.6 Refutation is not an incentive slogan

The Founder’s Edition urged falsification before confirmation. Version 2 keeps the adversarial intent but rejects a false sequence. Evidence can support, weaken, bound, or redirect a claim; good experiments often do several at once. Researchers should seek observations that discriminate among accounts, not perform hostility toward their own proposal as ritual.

Incentives matter because the proposer knows the design best and also has reason to defend it. Independent criticism, blinded or sealed evidence where appropriate, reproducible artifacts, and separate promotion authority reduce that conflict. None eliminates judgment. The Red Team can expose defects; it does not acquire unilateral authority to declare truth.

## 7.7 Failure as a durable result

A failed hypothesis contributes when the test was valid and the conclusion remains scoped. It may eliminate a parameter region, reveal an unstable representation, distinguish two mechanisms, or show that costs dominate an otherwise predictive relationship. The record should state what was learned and what remains unresolved.

Invalid attempts are preserved separately. Leakage or corrupted data may teach an engineering lesson but cannot falsify the market claim. This distinction protects the institution from accumulating false negatives under the honorable label of failure.

Hypotheses make inquiry accountable because they create an object that can change state without changing history. Mechanisms make it generative because they connect one result to further predictions. Falsification makes it corrigible because claims remain exposed to evidence strong enough to alter them. Together they define scientific progress without pretending that science is an automatic verdict machine.
