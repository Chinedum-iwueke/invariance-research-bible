# Chapter 15 — The Invariants

An invariant is a constraint on institutional identity: a commitment that survives changes in markets, strategies, people, models, vendors, and implementations. It is not a prediction that the world will remain stable, an empirical regularity, or a slogan that overrides judgment.

The Founder’s Edition named fifteen invariants. Version 2 preserves every substantive commitment while separating constitutional boundaries from scientific principles, mission commitments, and operating heuristics. The complete mapping appears in [Appendix A](../appendices/a-constitutional-principles.md).

## 15.1 Reality constrains belief

The institution changes empirical belief in response to applicable evidence. Observation can be incomplete, contaminated, or misinterpreted, so evidence reaches belief through provenance, criticism, and governed review. Status cannot substitute for evidence, and rhetoric cannot substitute for measurement.

## 15.2 Knowledge is the institutional product

Invariance Research exists to produce reliable, reusable knowledge about markets and decisions under uncertainty. Profit is necessary to sustain a capital-bearing institution but does not validate scientific quality by itself. Negative results, boundaries, and corrected errors can be valuable products.

## 15.3 Questions and representation precede optimization

The scientific question defines the object of inquiry. Representation determines how that object is observed. Performance optimization follows an explicit question, hypothesis, and evidence plan. Safety, reliability, and operational optimization remain legitimate independent duties.

## 15.4 Claims remain exposed and revisable

Hypotheses are registered before privileged evaluation and designed to encounter adverse and discriminating evidence. Empirical and implementation beliefs remain corrigible. Constitutional doctrine changes only through explicit canonical governance.

## 15.5 Memory preserves lineage under governance

Every material experiment, failure, decision, deployment, incident, and engineering task receives a disposition and returns durable knowledge to Research Intelligence. Provenance, access, retention, privacy, security, and lawful deletion constrain memory. Preservation never authorizes indiscriminate collection.

## 15.6 Authority remains separated

Exploration, evaluation, governance, and promotion are distinct authorities. No proposer, agent, model, or service promotes its own output. Urgent risk controls may act within delegated authority without rewriting the scientific record.

## 15.7 Institutional cognition remains external to replaceable models

Research Intelligence is the institutional brain, corpus, reasoning substrate, and long-term memory. Hermes is the governed operating system and organization. Bulletproof BT is the deterministic experimental laboratory. Model runtimes, vendors, and individual participants are replaceable; institutional authority and memory are not stored solely within them.

## 15.8 Deterministic services govern calculation and transition

Models may propose, summarize, criticize, and predict. Versioned deterministic services remain authoritative for governed calculation, simulation, risk verdicts, evidence production, and state transition. A model output is not primary evidence merely because it is fluent or confident.

## 15.9 Evidence environments remain distinct

Backtest, robustness analysis, shadow, execution replay, demo, micro-live, and production answer different scientific and operational questions. Evidence cannot be promoted by renaming an environment. Profit in one environment does not establish eligibility for another.

## 15.10 Scope is explicit and profit is not guaranteed

Crypto markets are the initial model organism, not the permanent boundary of the institution. Scientific and architectural contracts remain market-agnostic where evidence permits. No doctrine, architecture, model, or process guarantees profitable alpha. Costs, competition, capacity, uncertainty, and change remain real constraints.

## 15.11 Applying an invariant

An invariant constrains design and decisions but does not compute them. When invariants appear to conflict, governance identifies the applicable claims, consequences, and authority. Safety containment can precede completed science; lawful deletion can constrain memory; a useful prediction can operate without a complete causal mechanism. The decision record explains the reconciliation.

Operating principles and heuristics remain subordinate. Weak edges may combine but need not. Search is scarce alongside attention, data, compute, and capital. Learning remains open-ended while bounded work must finish. These principles guide action without being misrepresented as permanent facts about markets.

The invariants protect the conditions under which the institution can correct itself. Their purpose is not to preserve specific answers. It is to preserve scientific identity while answers, systems, and environments change.
