# Chapter 11 — Non-Stationarity and Adaptive Markets

Historical evidence is conditional on the process that generated it. Markets change as participants learn, rules change, technology alters access, capital moves, and the institution itself acts. Invariance Research therefore treats stability as a claim to be tested, not a property granted by a long sample.

This stance is compatible with complexity economics and the Adaptive Markets Hypothesis, which emphasize feedback, heterogeneity, and evolutionary change ([Arthur, 1999](../../../bibliography/references.md#arthur-1999-complexity); [Lo, 2004](../../../bibliography/references.md#lo-2004-adaptive-markets)). These frameworks motivate inquiry; they do not prove that every pattern decays or that every change has a recoverable participant-level explanation.

## 11.1 Conditional knowledge

An empirical claim states the population, period, venue, rules, participants, horizon, representation, and decision context to which its evidence applies. Transport beyond that scope is another claim. A relationship observed in one environment may persist, weaken, reverse, or become economically irrelevant elsewhere.

Conditionality does not make knowledge useless. It makes the boundary of use explicit. Approximate local stability can support forecasting and decision-making when the stability assumption is monitored and the consequences of failure are bounded.

> **Doctrine B1-PR10 — Stability is evidenced**  
> Every use of historical regularity identifies and monitors the environmental conditions under which that regularity is expected to remain decision-relevant.

## 11.2 Change has several layers

Distribution shift can affect inputs, targets, conditional relationships, or participant populations. Representation semantics can change when a venue revises messages or an index changes construction. Costs and fill behavior can change while prediction remains stable. Infrastructure changes can alter latency or ordering. Portfolio interaction can change when other positions or market participants adapt.

These are different failures. A single “drift score” cannot diagnose them all. Monitoring attaches to the scientific and operational objects whose assumptions can break: data, representation, mechanism, estimator, decision rule, execution, cost, dependence, and control.

Book VI owns model and feature drift; Book VIII owns venue, liquidity, and execution drift; Book VII owns portfolio dependence. Research Intelligence preserves the shared evidence without collapsing these authorities.

## 11.3 Regimes are models

Regime language can summarize recurring market conditions, but regimes are not self-announcing natural kinds. A regime label depends on variables, scale, method, and purpose. Discrete labels may be useful even when change is continuous; continuous state models may be useful even when rules create abrupt breaks.

A regime model therefore states its observables, causal availability, transition assumptions, uncertainty, and decision use. Post hoc labels fitted to explain performance are descriptive evidence, not prospective state detection. The institution evaluates whether a regime-conditioned rule improves decisions after accounting for estimation and search.

## 11.4 Decay and adaptation

An edge can decay through competition, dissemination, capacity, institutional change, or loss of the condition that supported it. It can also appear to decay because measurement, implementation, or costs changed. “Alpha half-life” is meaningful only under a defined decay model; not every edge follows exponential decline or has a stable half-life.

Adaptation is an institutional response, not an instruction to retrain continuously. Updating too quickly can chase noise, contaminate evaluation, and erase diagnostic evidence. Updating too slowly can preserve obsolete behavior. Cadence follows the mechanism, evidence arrival, risk, and cost of error.

Every update creates a new version with a stated trigger, evidence window, protected evaluation, and rollback path. Performance before and after change remains distinguishable.

## 11.5 Prospective monitoring

Historical robustness cannot observe future environment change. Prospective monitoring records predictions, exposures, expected uncertainty, realized outcomes, costs, state indicators, and operational events as time unfolds. Shadow and later environments supply different parts of that record.

Monitoring thresholds are not automatic scientific verdicts. A breach can trigger containment, review, demotion, or a registered investigation. Urgent risk authority may act before causal diagnosis, while the scientific record remains honest about what is known.

## 11.6 The invariant within change

The institution does not promise permanent strategies. Its durable commitment is to preserve lineage, test stability, detect consequential change, respond under authority, and learn from retirement as carefully as from promotion. The invariants govern how claims are handled even when their content changes.

Non-stationarity limits extrapolation; it does not abolish disciplined inference. By treating stability, transport, and adaptation as explicit claims, Invariance Research can act under change without pretending that yesterday’s evidence is either eternal truth or worthless history.
