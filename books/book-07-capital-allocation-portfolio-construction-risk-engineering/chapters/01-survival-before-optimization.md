# Chapter 1 — Survival Before Optimization

Capital allocation begins by preserving the institution’s ability to continue. Expected growth, diversification, and opportunity matter only inside limits that prevent ordinary error, model failure, market discontinuity, and operational incident from becoming terminal.

## 1.1 Survival object

Survival is mandate-specific. Define loss absorption, liquidity needs, leverage and margin, maximum authorized drawdown, concentration, legal and account rules, recovery time, and actions available during stress. A limit is not a forecast of the worst possible loss; it is a boundary for exposure and response.

## 1.2 Three layers

Separate hard constraints, risk budgets, and optimization preferences. Hard constraints reject actions. Risk budgets ration permitted exposure. Preferences choose among feasible portfolios. An optimizer may not trade off a hard limit against expected return.

## 1.3 Uncertainty

Expected return is usually estimated less precisely than risk and cost. Dependence changes in stress, liquidity disappears, and model errors correlate. The system discounts forecasts, uses uncertainty sets and conservative costs, limits leverage, and maintains slack.

## 1.4 Growth sizing

Log-growth arguments such as [Kelly 1956](../../../bibliography/references.md#kelly-1956) formalize a relationship between probabilities, payoffs, and long-run growth in a stylized repeated setting. Applying full estimated Kelly fractions to uncertain, dependent, non-stationary returns can create severe drawdowns. The institution treats growth-optimal sizing as analysis under assumptions, not an automatic target; fractional and capped variants still require stress evidence.

## 1.5 Risk as control

Risk engineering includes prevention, detection, response, recovery, and learning. It specifies who can halt trading, how exposure is reduced, which data and systems are authoritative, and how incidents feed back into model and portfolio decisions.

> **Risk rule B7-RK01 — Feasibility precedes optimality**  
> Capital enters optimization only after hard mandate, liquidity, margin, concentration, loss, and operational constraints define a survivable feasible set.
