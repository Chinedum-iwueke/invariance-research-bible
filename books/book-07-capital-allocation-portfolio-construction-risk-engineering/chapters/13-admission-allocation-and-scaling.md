# Chapter 13 — Admission, Allocation, and Scaling

Capital progresses through distinct decisions: scientific admission, portfolio eligibility, initial allocation, scaling, maintenance, and removal. Strong evidence at one stage does not authorize the next.

## 13.1 Initial allocation

New candidates receive bounded capital based on evidence quality, forecast and risk uncertainty, dependence, costs, capacity, operational state, and reversibility. Short history limits diversification credit and scaling speed.

## 13.2 Scaling ladder

Stages can include zero-capital shadow, constrained demo, micro-live, limited production, and mature allocation. Each stage has minimum duration or event count, acceptance metrics, loss and incident limits, maximum step size, and rollback.

Scaling evidence includes prospective forecast calibration, realized costs, capacity, portfolio interaction, execution parity, system health, and absence of unexplained discrepancies. P&L alone is insufficient.

## 13.3 Competition for capital

Candidates compete on marginal portfolio contribution after costs, risk, dependencies, and constraints. Existing allocations are not entitled to permanence. A new candidate can displace capital only through the governed allocator and authority process.

## 13.4 Conditions and expiry

Admission and allocation decisions carry conditions, owner, effective time, review date, and expiry. A model or strategy version change can require re-admission even when the name is unchanged.

## 13.5 Attribution

Scaling decisions link to evidence available at the time. Later outcomes are evaluated against that record so the institution can learn whether its sizing process—not merely the strategy—was sound.

> **Risk rule B7-RK13 — Capital scales by evidence and reversibility**  
> Increasing exposure requires prospective, portfolio-aware, execution-aware evidence and a tested path to reduce exposure safely.
