## Chapter 1–6 evidence notes

### Chapter 1

The chapter establishes the institution—not a strategy—as the enduring unit; organized knowledge—not information volume—as intellectual capital; and reliable discovery—not direct profit optimization—as the governing process objective. It assigns Research Intelligence the conversion of evidence into organizational knowledge and treats deployment as continued observation. These are constitutional claims. Statements that knowledge “appreciates through use,” that every experiment contributes permanently, or that profit follows scientific excellence require qualification: unusable records, invalid experiments, and adverse economics do not create value automatically. The final page contains first-person editorial conversation (“I have to say…”, “I think…”); it is production residue and will be archived from canonical prose.

### Chapter 2

Discovery is defined as a reliable increase in knowledge that changes future research decisions. The chapter distinguishes search from discovery, treats negative findings as valuable, makes belief provisional, and requires mechanisms, replication, criticism, and records. Its strongest correction is that markets do not owe the institution alpha. Claims about scientific practice and market opportunity must be separated into institutional doctrine, philosophy-of-science argument, and empirical propositions.

### Chapter 3

The market is presented as a partially observed adaptive network whose price series is an output rather than a complete state description. The chapter supplies the constitutional basis for representation-first research: every transformation exposes some structure and suppresses other structure. It also makes time horizon, costs, and execution assumptions part of the hypothesis. Named components such as the Representation Agent and Opportunity Graph are later-system specifications and should appear only as consequences here.

### Chapter 4

The chapter distinguishes research from trading by object, horizon, success criterion, and authority. This distinction supports separation of exploration, evaluation, governance, and capital promotion. Book I should establish why the separation is constitutional; Book II should define decision rights and records.

### Chapter 5

The chapter develops knowledge compounding through preserved experiments, explanations, failures, and cross-domain connection. Version 2 will retain the doctrine while rejecting automatic compounding: evidence has value only when provenance, validity, retrieval, and future relevance are maintained. Book III owns the architecture that makes this possible.

### Chapter 6

The chapter subordinates optimization to prior scientific question formation and separates discovery, validation, and production clocks. It makes discovery an institutional capability rather than episodic inspiration. Its absolute instruction to prefer future discovery over local improvement needs a governed exception for safety, reliability, legal duty, and urgent operational risk. Search services, named graphs, agents, and compute policy move to Books II and IV.

## Terminology candidates from chapters 1–6

`organized knowledge`, `knowledge-producing system`, `discovery`, `negative result`, `market structure`, `representation`, `mechanism`, `Discovery Clock`, `Validation Clock`, `Production Clock`, `Opportunity Graph`, `Curiosity Graph`, `Question Graph`, and `Continuous Research Agenda` require glossary adjudication. The three “clocks” are useful conceptual cadences but must not be confused with the five validation environments or physical time semantics.

## Chapter 7–12 evidence notes

### Chapter 7

Weak Edge Theory rejects a search for a single perfect strategy in favor of multiple modest, independently useful contributions. Breadth across reasonable parameters, instruments, regimes, and cost assumptions is treated as stronger evidence than an isolated optimum; parameter plateaus are preferred to sharp peaks. Version 2 must avoid turning these heuristics into universal scientific laws: broad false effects exist, genuine mechanisms can be narrow, and informational independence is not equivalent to statistical independence. Portfolio value and capacity belong to Book VII; the doctrine of accumulation remains in Book I.

### Chapter 8

The chapter treats institutional attention as the scarce resource allocated by research. It introduces differential question value, negative information, expected information gain, discovery return on investment, and research as a portfolio of investigations. The governing insight is retained, but formulas or rankings must represent uncertain decision support rather than an objective oracle. Opportunity Graph implementation and research-budget policy move to Books IV and II respectively.

### Chapter 9

Questions determine the search space, assumptions, relevant evidence, and possible mechanisms. The chapter assigns questions a lifecycle and introduces a graph of relationships among unanswered problems. Version 2 retains question-first and anomaly-first doctrine. Agent roles, question scoring, and lifecycle implementation move to Books II, IV, and IX.

### Chapter 10

The hypothesis—not an idea, model, or profitable configuration—is the registered unit of scientific progress. A hypothesis must expose itself to failure, state a mechanism and null, include representation choices, and precede privileged evaluation. Machine learning is a method within an experimental claim, not the hypothesis itself. The “burden of proof” language will be calibrated to statistical evidence rather than legal metaphor; formal contracts and lifecycle states belong to Books IV and V under Book II governance.

### Chapter 11

The chapter rejects equal confidence across sources and proposes an evidence pyramid, explicit confidence, contextual relevance, contradiction preservation, and evidence that flows into later decisions. Its essential contribution is contextual authority rather than a universal linear hierarchy. The Founder’s Edition headings “Books Are Not Authority” and “Papers Are Beginning Points” need revision: books and papers can be authoritative for particular claims, while no source is authoritative outside its scope. Book III owns the evidence model and confidence records.

### Chapter 12

Institutional memory preserves reasoning, assumptions, relationships, revision history, failures, and operational evidence across changes in people, models, software, and hardware. It is networked, generative, and evolutionary rather than a file archive. The five Founder’s Edition memory categories—Foundational, Scientific, Institutional, Operational, and Reflective—conflict partly with the later architecture, which defines four knowledge classes and six functional memory forms. Version 2 will preserve the useful distinctions but adopt the later controlled taxonomy: knowledge authority and cognitive memory function are separate axes. Claims that every experiment becomes permanently valuable will be qualified by validity, retention, cost, and governance.

## Chapter 13–18 evidence notes

### Chapter 13

Representation is established as observational and scientific design rather than neutral preprocessing. The chapter correctly distinguishes market reality from trades, quotes, candles, and other compressed measurements; ties representation to purpose and hypothesis; and subjects learned representations to the same validation burden as handcrafted transforms. Fractional differentiation is a useful example, not a constitutional default: its memory and stationarity properties are parameter-, test-, and series-dependent and require primary citations. The Representation Agent and Representation Graph move to Books IV, VI, and IX, while Book V owns deterministic fit/transform and provenance contracts.

### Chapter 14

Portfolio thinking is extended upstream from positions to beliefs, mechanisms, questions, hypotheses, representations, methods, and institutional attention. The distinction between return correlation and shared explanatory dependence is valuable, but causal stories cannot substitute for measured statistical dependence and joint stress behavior. “Knowledge Portfolio” is retained as an institutional planning abstraction, not treated as a financial portfolio with directly commensurable value. Capital construction belongs to Book VII; agent diversity belongs to Book IX.

### Chapter 15

The Discovery Engine is a continuous institutional loop from observation through registered hypothesis, representation, experiment, independent criticism, prospective evidence, memory, and renewed questions. It clearly separates the institution from any single model and makes hypotheses versioned scientific objects. Its listed requirements—purged validation, immutable datasets, walk-forward analysis, multiplicity correction, and named agent sequence—are candidate controls rather than universally applicable ingredients; experiment design must select methods appropriate to the claim. The Founder’s Edition loop skips execution replay and demo between shadow and micro-live, a gap superseded by the later five-environment architecture.

### Chapter 16

The chapter’s durable principle is organizational before computational architecture: departments and governed capabilities outlive particular agents and runtimes. It requires structured opposition, trial registration, immutable holdouts, independent promotion, institutional incentives for falsification, and culture that values correction. The mature “digital faculty” is target architecture, not current capability. The statement that Hermes is the faculty and Research Intelligence merely the library conflicts with ADR-0002; Version 2 will use the canonical distinction that Hermes is the operating system and organization while Research Intelligence is the brain, corpus, and memory. Anthropomorphic promises that agents never forget or continuously read everything will be removed.

### Chapter 17

The Daily Research Cycle provides a useful cadence of observation, prioritization, experimentation, criticism, reflection, and memory publication. Its concrete claims about overnight ingestion, thousands of instruments, automatic drift analysis, continuous shadow operation, and daily graph updates are unimplemented target behavior. The chapter also conflates shadow with simulated orders and omits execution replay and demo. Full operational treatment moves to Book II; Book I retains only the constitutional requirement that activity becomes knowledge through reflection and provenance. “No day shall conclude until the institution has increased understanding” is not an auditable or always achievable invariant and will become an aspiration constrained by honest null outcomes and operational duty.

### Chapter 18

The “cortical” metaphor decomposes Hermes into research, mathematics, statistics, representation, ML, market structure, experimentation, adversarial review, portfolio, governance, and memory capabilities communicating through structured scientific objects. Its strongest design rules survive: specialization, independent review, replaceable models, shared durable memory, and typed artifacts rather than arbitrary prompt exchange. The named cortex hierarchy is superseded as the top-level organizational architecture by the five Hermes organizations in `PROJECT_CONTEXT_FOR_CODEX.md`; it may remain as an explanatory capability projection, not a second authority tree. Research Intelligence—not a Memory Cortex—is the canonical brain and memory, and Bulletproof BT is a laboratory service rather than a cortical region.

## Chapter 19–24 evidence notes

### Chapter 19

Learning is distinguished from storage: institutional evidence counts as learning only when it produces a traceable change in questions, confidence, methods, representations, or decisions. The chapter develops hierarchical belief, concept formation, analogy, transfer, failure analysis, meta-learning, curricula, theory formation, revision, and institutional plasticity. The principle is sound, but “every experiment must change the institution” is too strong; a valid null update may rationally leave a belief approximately unchanged while still adding lineage and constraining future synthesis. The five-level hierarchy places doctrine as learned knowledge, whereas doctrine changes require explicit constitutional governance rather than ordinary belief updating. “Learning Graph” should be a temporal projection over evidence and belief events, not an independently mutable truth store.

### Chapter 20

Markets are treated as complex adaptive systems characterized by heterogeneous participants, feedback, nonlinear response, partial information, regimes, path dependence, and emergence. This supplies a productive research stance but contains external empirical and theoretical claims that require authoritative sources; the metaphor cannot establish that every market phenomenon has a recoverable participant-level mechanism. “Adaptation destroys permanence” and “no participant dominates permanently” are unjustified absolutes. The proposed Synthetic Market Laboratory is deferred capability and belongs to Book IV; simulations can test implications of specified mechanisms but cannot validate that their artificial ecology is the market.

### Chapter 21

The chapter distinguishes realized profit, benchmark exposure, leverage, luck, predictive information, and implementation. It defines alpha as conditional information about future market behavior that survives uncertainty, competition, costs, and prospective observation. A material internal conflict follows: section 21.7 declares alpha independent of implementation, while the controlled definition requires survival after execution costs and capacity. Version 2 will distinguish **predictive edge** from **realizable alpha** and from **realized active return**. Discovery may precede trading, but a relationship cannot be called realizable alpha until implementation feasibility and net economics are evidenced. Financial performance does not by itself “validate scientific quality.”

### Chapter 22

The chapter classifies informational, behavioral, structural, liquidity, execution, temporal, cross-sectional, representation, statistical, ML, portfolio, governance, and research “alpha.” Several categories conflate sources of predictive edge, methods of extraction, implementation improvements, institutional capabilities, and realized portfolio outcomes. Governance and research discipline may improve discovery quality but are not market alpha. Representation and ML are often methods, not causal sources. Version 2 will replace the flat Alpha Atlas with a typed opportunity taxonomy: mechanism, observable information, representation, estimator, decision rule, execution advantage, portfolio interaction, and institutional process remain distinct.

### Chapter 23

Discovery is described as allocation across a combinatorial search space of questions, representations, horizons, instruments, methods, and parameters. Expected information gain and explanation-oriented search are useful priorities. Search is not literally capital allocation, and automated scheduling cannot estimate scientific value with objective precision. Search-history accounting, multiplicity, protected holdouts, stopping rules, and budgeted autonomy are underdeveloped here and belong to Book IV under Book II governance.

### Chapter 24

The chapter separates risk from uncertainty, confidence from certainty, epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty, and robust decisions from narrow precision. It preserves unknowns, contradictions, alternative futures, calibration, and error records. The discussion of “95% confidence” must not imply a single generic interpretation: frequentist confidence intervals, Bayesian credible probabilities, predictive coverage, and institutional belief scores are different objects. Aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty can also be model-relative rather than perfectly separable. The Uncertainty Graph should be an evidence-linked projection containing typed uncertainty and scope, not a scalar map of “ignorance.”

## Chapter 25–30 evidence notes

### Chapter 25

The chapter adopts non-stationarity and market evolution as default research risks, while allowing approximate local stability. It expands drift beyond ML features to targets, representations, costs, execution, infrastructure, mechanisms, and institutional assumptions. This is a valuable monitoring doctrine, but claims that every alpha has a half-life, regimes are continuous rather than discrete, and markets violate stability more than almost any scientific system are empirical or model-dependent—not constitutional facts. Shadow is correctly treated as prospective scientific evidence, yet the text wrongly includes simulated execution within shadow. Evolution Graph, continuous retraining, and automated drift monitoring are target proposals; Book VI owns model drift and Book VIII owns execution/venue drift.

### Chapter 26

Information theory is used to connect entropy, mutual information, representation, features, expected information gain, decay, and communication. Version 2 must sharply separate Shannon quantities from metaphor. Entropy applies to a specified random variable and probability model; mutual information describes statistical dependence and does not itself establish economic value, causality, executability, or alpha. “Alpha reduces entropy,” “information efficiency,” and “information portfolio” are not self-defining technical propositions. The chapter should retain information preservation and uncertainty-reduction doctrine while relocating equations, estimation bias, conditional information, and validation to Books IV and VI with primary sources.

### Chapter 27

Correlation is treated as evidence that initiates explanation rather than completes it. The chapter requires competing mechanisms, assumptions, counterfactual reasoning, discriminating predictions, and revision history. This improves scientific discipline, but requiring a plausible mechanism for every statistically useful relationship would exclude legitimate predictive or risk-control models whose use can be bounded and empirically validated without causal identification. Version 2 will distinguish predictive, explanatory, and causal claim classes. Counterfactual questions alone are not causal identification, and observational finance does not make causal evidence impossible; natural experiments, quasi-experimental designs, structural models, and domain interventions have varying strengths. The Mechanism Graph remains an evidence-linked projection.

### Chapter 28

Scientific humility is made operational through precise language, explicit unknown states, dissent preservation, independent red teams, periodic challenge, versioned beliefs, model limitations, and willingness to revise. These commitments remain constitutional. The proposed belief states duplicate Book II’s promotion states and combine epistemic confidence with operational authorization; Version 2 will keep these as separate state machines. “Evidence above authority” does not erase source expertise or decision authority; it prevents status from substituting for applicable evidence. Capital sizing cannot be a direct scalar function of a generic confidence score and belongs to Book VII.

### Chapter 29

The chapter declares fifteen Founder’s Edition invariants. Many are durable commitments, but several are slogans, empirical generalizations, or capability priorities rather than irreducible identity constraints. Internal tensions require formal reconciliation: “every belief is provisional” includes the Doctrine, while the Invariants are described as permanent; “institutional memory is sacred” requires lawful retention and security exceptions; “falsification before confirmation” oversimplifies scientific evidence; “search is the scarcest resource” conflicts with Chapter 8’s attention thesis; and “weak edges compound into durable advantage” is not guaranteed. The [invariants register](../registers/INVARIANTS.md) records controlled treatment.

### Chapter 30

The manifesto forcefully preserves reality, scientific integrity, memory, governance, humility, and the absence of profit guarantees. Its short declarative form is appropriate for a charter but excessively repeats earlier maxims and contains absolutes that cannot function as technical rules. Hermes is accurately distinguished from an LLM or swarm, although the phrase “Hermes is an evolving scientific institution” needs the canonical formulation: Hermes is the institution’s governed operating system and organization. The closing assertion that everything after Book I is implementation conflicts with the adopted series architecture: Books II and III remain Constitutional Knowledge governing organization and cognition. Version 2 will close with the controlled declaration and cross-volume dependency rather than a false philosophy/engineering divide.

## Book I audit conclusion

All thirty Founder’s Edition chapters have received a structural disposition and evidence review. Version 2 should be shorter because implementation specifications and repeated maxims move to their authoritative books, but it must preserve the developed arguments behind institutional purpose, discovery, representation, falsification, uncertainty, memory, and humility. Compression is not the objective; coherent constitutional scope is.
