# Controlled Glossary

**Status:** Initial controlled register  
**Rule:** Canonical terms are capitalized as shown when naming institutional systems or knowledge classes. Synonyms may explain a term but may not silently replace it.

**Bulletproof BT.** The deterministic, event-driven experimental laboratory and evidence-producing instrument of Invariance Research. It is not synonymous with Hermes or Research Intelligence.

**Anomaly.** A scoped deviation from an explicit expectation supplied by a model, mechanism, benchmark, prior distribution, operational contract, or established institutional belief. An anomaly is not, by itself, a signal or opportunity.

**Causal claim.** A claim about how an intervention, treatment, or counterfactual changes an outcome under an explicit identification strategy and assumptions. Predictive association alone does not establish a causal claim.

**Candidate maturity.** The governed readiness state of a candidate—such as registered, screened, replicated, robust, portfolio eligible, or production eligible—kept separate from epistemic state, validation environment, and operational state.

**Bounded autonomy.** Delegated freedom to select actions within an explicit charter of objectives, authority, resources, data access, prohibitions, stopping rules, and required records. Bounded autonomy does not include authority to expand the charter or approve one’s own outputs.

**Control.** A mechanism that prevents, permits, detects, or records a consequential action under a versioned policy. A control is not proven effective until its behavior is tested or observed.

**Decision right.** An explicit institutional grant to make a specified decision for defined objects, environments, limits, prerequisites, and duration. Capability to act does not imply a decision right.

**Constitutional Knowledge.** The highest-priority institutional policy layer: the Bible, controlled doctrines, glossary, and adopted architectural rules.

**Demo.** Validation through a simulated or paper venue’s real API and order lifecycle, without real capital. Demo tests engineering integration, not prospective scientific validity.

**Deterministic service.** A versioned, testable component authoritative for calculation, simulation, risk verdicts, state transitions, or evidence production given explicit inputs.

**Evidence dossier.** A provenance-preserving assembly of relevant claims, contradictions, sources, experiments, and limitations prepared for a bounded decision or research task.

**Evidence.** Information used to support or weaken a specified claim under an explicit relationship, scope, and method. Evidence is not synonymous with information, a citation, or a model output.

**Explanatory claim.** A claim that a specified process or mechanism accounts for an observed relationship and yields discriminating implications. A plausible narrative alone does not establish an explanatory claim.

**Execution replay.** Validation that passes recorded or live-captured events through production-equivalent decision, risk, order-management, and adapter logic without venue submission. Replay fills remain modeled evidence.

**Hermes.** The governed research operating system and organization of Invariance Research. Hermes coordinates authority, people, agents, deterministic services, evidence, workflows, and decisions; it is not “the AI.”

**Implementation fact.** A claim verified against identified code, tests, schemas, documentation, or artifacts at a specified version.

**Institutional Knowledge.** Hypotheses, trials, experiments, failures, postmortems, decisions, and other records of what Invariance has learned.

**Institutional belief.** A versioned, attributable assessment of a claim that records its scope, evidential basis, uncertainty, review state, and revision history. It is not a timeless fact or a model’s transient output.

**Invalid attempt.** A run, trial, or experiment whose scientific interpretation is blocked by leakage, corruption, implementation failure, violated assumptions, or another recorded validity defect. An invalid attempt is not a negative result.

**Knowledge class.** One of the four authority-and-origin categories used by Research Intelligence: Constitutional Knowledge, Scientific Knowledge, Institutional Knowledge, and Operational Knowledge.

**Memory form.** One of the six functional uses of institutional memory: semantic, episodic, procedural, working, executive, or reflective. Memory forms do not replace knowledge classes.

**Mechanism.** A specified process through which a proposed relationship could arise, together with boundary conditions and implications that can distinguish it from competing explanations.

**Micro-live.** A validation environment using deliberately tiny real capital to observe real venue, liquidity, latency, fill, reconciliation, and operational behavior. It is not production eligibility.

**Model organism.** A deliberately bounded initial domain used to learn quickly while preserving general architectural boundaries. Liquid crypto spot and perpetual markets are the initial model organism.

**Model runtime.** A replaceable inference engine used by a cognitive service. Its parameters are not the institution’s durable memory or constitutional authority.

**Operational Knowledge.** System documentation, ADRs, RFCs, runbooks, incidents, deployments, and shadow/demo/live operational records.

**Operational state.** The current service or deployment condition—such as ready, running, paused, degraded, halted, or recovering—kept separate from scientific belief, candidate maturity, and validation environment.

**Operating policy.** A versioned institutional rule defining actors, scope, required evidence, conditions, exceptions, accountable authority, enforcement, and effective interval.

**Predictive edge.** Evidence that information available at a decision time changes the conditional distribution of a relevant future outcome. A predictive edge is not yet realizable alpha because costs, capacity, constraints, and executable decisions may eliminate its economic value.

**Predictive claim.** A claim that information available at a defined decision time changes the conditional distribution of a specified future outcome within a stated scope. A predictive claim need not assert causal identification.

**Production.** Capital-bearing operation by a governance-approved, portfolio-eligible strategy under assigned risk budgets, monitoring, and demotion or retirement controls.

**Representation.** A causal, versioned transformation or encoding of observations into a state suitable for a scientific question. Representation choice is part of the hypothesis, not neutral preprocessing.

**Realizable alpha.** A conditional informational advantage for which prospective evidence supports positive economic value after applicable costs, execution constraints, capacity, uncertainty, and competition. Realizable alpha is not the same as a realized profit observation.

**Realized active return.** The observed return attributable relative to a specified benchmark and accounting convention. It is an outcome affected by exposure, sizing, leverage, execution, costs, and chance; it is not sufficient evidence of predictive edge or realizable alpha.

**Research Intelligence.** The institutional brain, corpus, reasoning substrate, and long-term memory. It contains multiple knowledge and memory classes and uses replaceable models through governed cognitive services.

**Research question.** A bounded statement of the object, scope, uncertainty, and decision relevance of an inquiry, sufficient to constrain representations, evidence, methods, and interpretation.

**Search history.** The attributable record of candidate families, specifications, parameter variations, screens, failed or invalid attempts, result views, holdout exposures, and post-result adaptations relevant to interpreting a research result.

**Scientific Knowledge.** External books, papers, standards, mathematics, empirical literature, and methods, with provenance and authority appropriate to scientific claims.

**Shadow.** Prospective observation in which signals and expected outcomes are recorded against unfolding markets but no order is submitted. Shadow tests scientific expectations, not venue integration.

**Trial registration.** The immutable or append-only pre-evaluation record of a hypothesis, mechanism, design, metrics, exclusions, search budget, stopping rules, and holdout policy.

**Typed artifact.** A versioned institutional object with an explicit schema or contract, identity, lineage, authority, and allowed lifecycle. Narrative may be part of a typed artifact where structured fields cannot express the required reasoning.

**Uncertainty.** A typed limitation on what can be inferred or predicted, arising from sources such as sampling, measurement, model form, parameter estimation, environment change, implementation, dependence, or unmodeled alternatives. It is not always reducible to one scalar probability.

**Validation environment.** The environment in which evidence is produced—backtest, robustness analysis, shadow, execution replay, demo, micro-live, or production—recorded independently of candidate maturity and operational state.

**Veto.** A scoped authority to prevent a specified action when a stated requirement fails. A veto record identifies its evidence, authority, affected action, remediation path, and appeal route.
